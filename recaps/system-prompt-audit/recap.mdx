# About a third of what I read every turn is a second or third copy of something I have already read, and six rules point me the wrong way

**Fixed cost per request: about 30K tokens. The sidecar shows 19K of it (14,200 words); the other 11K is the 52 tool schemas, sent with every request and invisible in the capture.** Of twelve sections reviewed: three are worth keeping as they are, four should be trimmed, three rewritten, two cut. The pattern behind the waste is not "teaching the model to suck eggs" so much as **triple-statement**: a loom tool's contract is written once as its schema description, again as the one-line snippet in the tools block, and again as ambient guidelines in the rules block. The authoring doctrine prices the description as "selection-time" and the guideline as "ambient", but for the 52 resident tools both are sent on every turn, so the split that justifies the second copy does not hold. Egg-sucking is real but small: perhaps 1.5K tokens of "read: Read file contents" and "use session_search to find past sessions".

The harmful rules are fewer and more specific than the waste: a rule that tells me to log consults in `progress.md` in a repo that forbids scratch files; a tool whose default output location the project then spends a paragraph telling me not to use; "do not investigate the approach yourself" on a thread whose assignment is a question; a surface checklist naming mobile and six providers in a pi-only fork; and three places where the file states upstream's rule and then its override, so I read a negation to arrive at nothing.

If every recommendation lands: system prompt 19K to about 11K, schemas 11K to about 7K, so **roughly 30K to 18K per turn** at no behavioural bet I can identify. The figures are estimates at 1.35 tokens per word from one capture and a word count of the tool prose; the doctrine's own evidence bar (does the rule bind in real transcripts?) has not been run, and the two items that would benefit from it are marked.

## 3. The rules that point the wrong way, in one place

The second half of the question was whether any of this is over-prescriptive to the point of harm. Six, in order of how often they bite:

1. **"Do not investigate the approach yourself"** (G). Bites on every question-shaped root assignment; produces a cold-start spawn where a thought would do.
2. **Stated-then-overridden rules** (I). Bite on every turn as noise; on a bad turn I follow the upstream half ("CI owns the full suite") and skip `vp check`.
3. **"Hit every surface" naming mobile and six providers** (I). Bites on every frontend change: ceremony at best, edits to unbuilt code at worst.
4. **"Record every consult in progress.md"** (C). Bites rarely (consult_manager is rare in a workstream) but the outcome is a committed scratch file the project forbids.
5. **`visual_explainer` with its `~/.agent/diagrams/` default** (C). Bites whenever a human asks for a diagram: the tool writes somewhere the app cannot show, and the fix is a paragraph of counter-instruction.
6. **"authored only by a top-tier model… never author it"** (G). Does not bite yet; will the month the tiers roll and the absolute refers to the wrong model.

Not harmful, despite looking prescriptive: "substantial coders need review gates" (the same bullet exempts mechanical edits), "never `git stash`" (a real shared-`.git` hazard), "pass test paths directly, never after `--`" (a real trap), and the bash-sleep rule (bg_start exists for the reason it gives).
