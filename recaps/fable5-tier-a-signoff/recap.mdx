# Fable 5 tier-A taxonomy sign-off — condition ⇔ necessity

A verdict batch over **31 tier-(a) items** from the Fable 5 naive-taxonomy critique (§4.5–4.6). Each item is a place where the production lease taxonomy's own conditionality convention is internally inconsistent — a `relationship_role`'s `necessity` (or a `containment_context`'s `expected_presence`) and its `condition` field disagree about whether the edge is conditional. The `survey_plan` item (E-1) is a modelling question, not a mismatch. An assessor read every production pack in full and authored the exact replacement text; **nothing is applied yet** — your sign-off here is the gate, after which a deterministic apply step writes each accepted change verbatim into production.

<Callout tone="decision">
  **Review protocol.** Every item carries its verdict, the decisive before/after (`<FieldDiff>`), the verbatim production evidence (drill-down), and an **Accept / Reject / Discuss** control. **Silence means you accept the recommendation** — only record a choice on the items you want to reject or discuss. On sign-off, each accepted item's `Proposed` values are written verbatim into production; rejected/discussed items are held. The five genuinely balanced direction-calls the assessor flagged are gathered again as concrete either/or questions in **Balanced calls** at the foot of the document — answer those only if you prefer the alternative.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="info">
  **The convention being tested.** `condition` states *when* an edge applies and, per the schema, belongs on a `conditional` entry **and only there**. Two defect shapes: a **non-`conditional` level carrying a `condition`** (packs A, C) and a **`conditional` level missing its `condition`** (packs B, D). The verdict is never "just make the two fields line up" — it is a semantic call about which field is actually wrong. **NECESSITY_WRONG**: the edge really is conditional → promote the level, keep/author the condition. **CONDITION_WRONG**: the level is right → the "condition" is applicability/descriptive/tautological prose, so drop it or fold it into a description (or, for B/D, demote the level). **OTHER_DEFECT**: the mismatch is a red herring; the real defect is structural (E-1).
</Callout>
