# Five of sixteen bullets change; the gain is behaviour, not tokens

**873 words to about 700.** Eleven bullets stay as they are: the brief-writing craft, the human-hold rule, the defect-fix test, the hand-back test, the review posture. These are the sentences I consult in practice, and the audit found nothing to cut in them. The five that change do so because they point the wrong way, sit on the wrong surface, or name a model tier that will be wrong after the next platform update:

- **"Do not investigate the approach yourself"** (G1) contradicts the delegation test you set in F6 and, followed literally, spawns a planner for a question-shaped assignment. It goes; the F6 test decides.
- **"Delegate … investigation"** in the identity line (G0) goes for the same reason.
- **Gate recovery** (G6) is three sentences that bind only on the turn a gate yields, paid on every turn; the yield notice the control plane sends at that moment is where they belong, and the current notice is slightly at odds with them.
- **Model choice** (G9) names tiers ("top-tier", "Opus-level") that the monthly rollover moves; it should name the preset. It also carries the one rule whose authorship I could not trace, below.
- **MDX documents** (G13) keeps every instruction and loses a third of its words.

**One decision for you.** G9's sentence "Text that models will read … is authored only by a top-tier model … Opus-level children may research and review it, never author it" entered in commit `12f3791a7d` on 30 Sep with no thread trailer, so I cannot tell whether it is your decision or an agent's inference. It has been applied in practice (today's prose edits ran on the planner preset). If it is yours it stays, reworded to the preset; if not, it softens to a preference.

**Protocol:** an item left undecided takes its recommendation.
