# The `cat` guard would catch 6% of the problem; three other changes reach about half of it

In 14 days, agents on this machine pulled **24.8M tokens** into context through tool results of 20 KB or more: 11.5M through `bash` and the search tools, 13.3M through `read_full`. Judged turn by turn from two samples (50 bash and search results, 40 `read_full` results), **about 6.6M of that (27%) was avoidable**: output the caller could have bounded with a form it already knew, or a whole file read when one section was needed. The rest was deliberate reading of docs, source, diffs and reports, with no cheaper form that would not lose something. The incident that started this, dumping a data file to look up one entry, happened **three times in a fortnight**, all on `trust.json`, for about 80K tokens; a guard on it would also have blocked 54 legitimate file reads.

<Callout tone="decision" title="What it means now" focus>
  Of six items: **three to accept** (gate `read_full` above 100 KB behind an explicit `whole: true`; give the `rg` tool a files-only mode, a line-width cap and an honest description of `limit`; split the three upstream-sync runbooks so a stage's instructions are not inside a 500 KB ledger), **two to withdraw** (the `cat` guard; a blanket clip of bash output, which nets about 1.7M tokens against roughly 660 extra round trips a fortnight), and **one small habit** to record rather than legislate (hand an assessor an extracted transcript, not a raw session file). One question at the end: where the `rg` change lives, since the tool is a third-party pi extension. An item left undecided takes its recommendation.
</Callout>
