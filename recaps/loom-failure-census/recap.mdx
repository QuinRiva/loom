# Half of 40 recent threads needed a correction, and most of those were roots misjudging what to tell Carl

**20 of 40** sampled threads failed the census test ("did this thread do its job without a human correction, a rework round, a restart or an unverified claim"). Failure sits in the **roots**: **10 of 12** orchestrator roots failed against **10 of 28** children, and every researcher passed. The top three categories are **advice resting on a premise the agent never checked** (6 threads), **a hand-back Carl cannot act on without asking for an explanation** (6 threads) and **a risky step taken around a guard or a permission** (5 threads). The first two are judgement failures in roots facing Carl that no deterministic check can see; the third, and four smaller categories, are cheap structural checks for Eval 3.

  **Already said elsewhere.** Retro proposal "Explain product behaviour from source, not plausibility" (July); lessons of 29 October ("hand-audit a sample of the lost rows… before reporting the loss"; "test a hypothesis on the minimal case"), 1 November (check how stale a worktree is before reasoning about a live defect).

## 7. How the sample was drawn, and what it cannot tell you

- **Population.** 1,766 workstream threads whose record was created on or after 7 October 2026 (the orchestration re-platform), excluding this evals goal, threads started after 30 October (so they had finished), threads under $0.50 list price (under $0.20 for shippers) and threads with no pi session file.
- **Strata.** 12 orchestrator roots, 9 coders, 7 reviewers, 5 researchers, 4 planners and 3 shippers, close to each role's share of threads with roots over-weighted because Carl talks to them. 16 of the 40 were drawn from the **expensive tail** (the top 10% of goals by cost) against its 10% share of goals, on purpose. Reviewers were drawn so that 2 of 7 had a rework round, matching the 23% of reviewers that do. At most two threads per goal (three for shippers). Random within each stratum with a fixed seed (20261102).
- **Reading.** For each thread the retro scanner produced a digest over its tree (a root's whole tree; a child's parent and all siblings); four researcher children each coded ten threads from the digest, the brief, the reports and, where needed, the thread's own extract, under one written method, and returned one critique per thread with a verbatim quote. No thread was consulted. The categories were built once, over all 40 critiques, by this thread. Two dramatic claims (the production deletes and the public PR) were checked against the reports.
- **One sampling slip.** The Lantern House root (T01) was created on 29 September and re-recorded at the cut-over, so its quoted failures predate 7 October. No category depends on it: without it every row still has at least two threads.
- **Not Carl.** In the Doctree root (T02) the human writing was Jacob.
- **What it cannot tell you.** 40 threads see a failure that hits more than about 1 in 14 threads (94% chance) and miss rarer ones. The coding is one reader per thread with no second coder, so the boundary between categories 1, 4 and 5 (wrong fact, wrong intent, wrong size) is a judgement a second reader might draw differently. Tool friction a thread recovered from by itself was not coded, which is why the inbox's most repeated lesson (the `vp` PATH trap) and issue #356 (orchestrators re-enabling tools they hold) do not appear.
