# The loom tool schemas are 6,500 words a request, a third of it one contract written twice, and they read as rulebooks rather than contracts

**Measured from `LOOM_TOOL_DEFS` as pi serialises them: 22 tools, 3,909 words of description plus 2,579 words of parameter descriptions, about 8.5K tokens on every request of every thread that has them declared.** With the preamble switch the schema is each tool's only model-read surface, so what is cut here is simply gone; nothing moves to a second surface. Three things stand out:

- **`workstream_spawn` and `workstream_scaffold` are 1,876 of those words (29 percent)**, because scaffold's `nodes[]` item repeats spawn's twelve node parameters with their full descriptions (605 words). Both tools are declared to every orchestrator in the same request.
- **The five longest descriptions (prompt 349, spawn 325, request_attention 302, notify 269, submit 250) read as rulebooks**: capitalised NEVER and NOT, narration of what the result will say, and exceptions nested inside exceptions. This is the register your authoring rule names: lists of dos and don'ts written for a reader assumed to need them.
- **The sidecar does not capture this surface at all.** You audited 19K believing it was the whole prompt; the schemas were another 11K.

**What I am asking you to approve here is the rules and two worked samples, not 22 rewrites.** A rewrite of every description is a day of planner-preset work; approving its rules first means the result can be reviewed as a before/after batch rather than argued one tool at a time. The expected outcome, from the two samples as re-cut, is descriptions at roughly a quarter to a third of their size and parameters at two thirds; that is what the register produces, not a cap.

**Protocol:** an item left undecided takes its recommendation.

## 2. The rules

<Card id="k0" eyebrow="K0 · the register" heading="What a description is for, and what it is not" tone="accent" badge="approve the rules">

A description tells a capable reader three things and stops:

1. **What the tool does**, in its first sentence.
2. **The consequences the caller must act on**: what the call changes that the caller cannot undo or would not expect, and what the caller must do first. The test for every sentence, added after Carl's read of the two samples ("is all of the information there actually useful?"): **it stays only if it changes what the caller does or writes.** A mechanism whose consequence the result will report, or that the control plane handles either way, is dropped.
3. **When another tool is the better choice**, by name, in one clause each.

What goes, because the reader does not need it or the system already provides it:

- **Narration of the result** ("the result says whether it started, steered or queued", "the error names the offending key"). The result says it.
- **Capitalised prohibitions and lists of what the tool is NOT for.** `notify_thread` lists five. A reader who knows what `consult_thread`, `workstream_prompt` and `workstream_submit` do does not need to be told which of them `notify_thread` is not.
- **Timings and internals** ("within about a minute", "25 s slices", "BM25").
- **The same rule in the description and in a parameter.** Each rule lives in whichever of the two the reader meets first when it applies: a rule about a value is on the parameter; a rule about the call is in the description.
- **Doctrine.** Why gates exist, why a child starts cold, what a brief is for. That is the addendum's and the overlay's.

This is your rule applied to the one surface the fold left: "the models ingesting the content are very capable of making decisions, and overly prescriptive instructions constrain their ability to act intelligently."

## 3. Two worked rewrites, for the register

<Card id="k2" eyebrow="K2 · workstream_prompt · 349 → 86" heading="Does this say everything an orchestrator needs to prompt a child?" tone="info" badge="sample">

Re-cut after Carl's question, with the "changes what the caller does" test. Kept: what it does; the two facts that change how I write the message (an unlaunched child reads it as brief text; a stuck child must be stopped first); the hold-clearing consequence; the reopen rule as one clause. Dropped as mechanism the caller acts on nothing about: idle-versus-busy delivery, "a child with no brief is refused" (the refusal says so), "your message carries your authority, not the human's" (the child's addendum governs that), and everything the first cut had already removed.

<Columns>
  <Column label="Now (349 words)">
    Send a markdown message to a DIRECT child Workstream thread you spawned — you acting on the child as its parent. On an idle child (e.g. one you paused with mcp__t3-code__workstream_stop) this starts the next turn with your message — the resume path. On a busy child with an open turn it becomes a queued steer, folded in between model rounds. A child whose turn has not begun (just launched, still starting, or waiting on an answer) cannot take a steer yet: your message is queued, then steered in once the turn is up (within about a minute), or it starts the next turn if that one ends first; the result says whether it started, steered or queued. A steer canNOT penetrate a blocked/hung tool call — if the child is stuck inside a tool call, mcp__t3-code__workstream_stop it first, then prompt to restart it with guidance. Prompting a child that holds an attention flag (needs_guidance, awaiting_acceptance, or a yield awaiting you) CLEARS that hold when the turn starts, so prompt a flagged child only when you actually hold the answer or the approval it is waiting for; clearing it is your decision as the parent, not the human's approval, and is never reported upward as such. The child reads your message labelled as its parent orchestrator's — an agent's — which the human's messages outrank. On a child that has NOT started yet (briefed but waiting on a dependency, or briefed and not yet launched): your message is appended to its brief as an amendment and no turn starts — the child reads the brief plus every amendment as its first turn when the control plane launches it, and the result names what it is still waiting on. Amendments stack in order; a later mcp__t3-code__workstream_brief replaces the whole brief, amendments included. If the child has NO brief yet, the call is rejected with guidance to call mcp__t3-code__workstream_brief first. Prompting never launches a child: launch is the control plane's, once its dependencies are done. A finished child (outcome done or cancelled) is reopened with mcp__t3-code__workstream_set_outcome 'none' before you prompt it.

- **`workstream_submit`**: the routing cases (done, needs_human, review verdicts, unmatched tokens, rework rounds) stay, because a wrong token is a wrong routing; the quiescence warning ("a thread that ends its turn without submitting is treated as having gone quiet") stays as one sentence.
