# What "nothing there" looks like to the client, and what we propose

<Callout tone="info" title="This is a worked example, not a client record">
  The percentages and counts are from a real production run, but **every tenant, person, address, building id, quotation and lease-specific figure in this document and its screenshots is a fictional substitute**. "Mara", "Hadley Rowe Solicitors", "200 Marlow St" and the rest do not exist; the screenshots were edited to match. Nothing here should be read as a statement about a real tenant or a real client.
</Callout>

About **one in five** of the fields we show the client as acceptable have nothing behind them: no document, no clause. Not one of those verdicts was decided by a rule; every one is a sentence the AI writes, worded differently each run. That is Mara's complaint — and in **342 of 357** such cases the lease extraction had already written down the reason and the clause, which the audit throws away before anyone sees it.

###### Evidence: three of the client's buildings (200 Marlow St, Sydney and two others), 61 tenants, 28 audited fields each; production as it stood on 19 October. Click any screenshot to enlarge it.


<Callout tone="decision" title="Two directions, side by side" focus>
  <Badge tone="info">A</Badge> **Every field, with its reason.** The field stays; it reads "N/A · Physical make good", cites the clause, and counts as acceptable by rule. <Badge tone="accent">B</Badge> **A hierarchy decides what is audited.** Once we know the make good is physical, the payment amount is not audited at all: it sits collapsed under the make good type with the clause, out of the counts. Tenants with no lease get one finding, not 26 N/As. B is Carl's current leaning and the likely long-term direction; A is the design as written.

  In both, every empty field that _is_ audited carries its reason and document, the verdict comes from a fixed table where neither side has a figure, and the AI loses the power to make a field acceptable by deleting a value. **Choosing between A and B is this meeting's main question.**
</Callout>

<Callout tone="warning" title="The uncomfortable part">
  About one in six of today's empty Acceptables become Review or Investigate, because nothing was ever checked: tenants with no lease on file, incentive deeds we were never given. The client should hear that before the first re-run. It is the signal Mara asked for.
</Callout>

## 3. Why it happens

The audit was built to describe values. "Nothing there" was added later as a label on a value, so the reason never had anywhere to live. It falls out in the audit engine, the third step below.

<Steps>
  <Step at="1" title="Lease documents">
    Original Lease, clause 11.10(a): make good means reinstating the premises. No payment in lieu.
  </Step>
  <Step at="2" tone="success" title="Lease extraction" badge="reason kept">
    Writes the reason and the clause: `no payment amount is applicable`, cl 11.10, p.35. Cited.
  </Step>
  <Step at="3" tone="risk" title="Audit engine" badge="the reason falls out here">
    The audit only knows how to carry a value. An empty field is passed on as just "empty"; the reason and the clause are thrown away before the reviewer's screen is built.
  </Step>
  <Step at="4" title="AI adjudicator">
    Reads a prose note we wrote for it ("not applicable when the obligation is physical") and the extraction's working notes. Writes the verdict and a sentence.
  </Step>
  <Step at="5" title="Reviewer">
    Sees N/A, "No data", Acceptable, "No source data". The sentence is the only trace of the reason, and it is worded differently every run.
  </Step>
</Steps>

###### Today's path for Make Good Payment Amount, using Meridian (Harbinger Holdings) at 200 Marlow St.

<Details summary="More detail: how the engine mislabels what it does keep">
  The audit's own explanation of why a field is empty is often wrong. In 122 cases it reports "no entry found before the audit date" when dated entries exist and a hidden empty-value filter removed them. One label, "failed", covers a lease that has not started yet, a tenant whose documents are shaped differently from what the calculation expects, and a genuine software error (Orbis's "determined by independent quantity surveyor" crashed a number parser). Half of all the empty results are noise of the form "this tenant has no sublease" sitting beside the one line that matters.

  The lease extraction, by contrast, labels the same physical-make-good absence four different ways across tenants. So the kind of absence cannot be read off its label; it has to be decided from facts: an explicit "document not provided" marker, a date window, a governing term with a declared rule, a value that will not parse as a number, an explicit "not applicable" flag.
</Details>

## 7. Three ways forward

Keep patching field by field; rebuild so every field explains itself (A); or rebuild so the audit first decides which fields are worth auditing, then explains the rest (B). A and B share most of their work. The choice between them is the question for this meeting.

<Columns columns={3}>
  <Card tone="neutral" heading="Keep patching" badge="the lesser option" meta={["about a week per carve-out", "three more mechanisms, none removed"]}>
    Our engineer's open fix for Make Good Payment Amount, then the next carve-outs in the same style.

    - **Extend the make good fix to its sibling fields.** The engineer's fix cites the clause on 162 of the 674 cells, all on Make Good Payment Amount. Extending it to condition standard, base year, lessee proportion and the two outgoings amounts cites ≈140 more. The rule is written in code and in prose, six times over. The ERP side still shows nothing; the verdict is still the AI's.
    - **Merge the branch that keeps the explanation when the audit's calculation for a field ran but found no figure.** Improves ≈15 cells (formulas, unparsed ERP notes). The complaint's own case, where the calculation never ran because the lease side was empty, is unchanged.
    - **Make the verdict deterministic on the hooked fields.** Stable on those cells only; the AI can still delete values elsewhere.

    **After all three:** ≈320 of the 674 cells cited, ≈300 still prose-only, verdicts stable on ≈160. "Consistently across all fields" is not met.
  </Card>
  <Card tone="info" eyebrow="Direction A" heading="Every field, with its reason" badge="the design as written" meta={["four phases, ≈ four weeks", "less code than today", "one model for all 90 fields"]}>
    - **Phase 1: every empty field carries its reason and document.** Pipeline, UI, export and data store change together. All 674 cells cited; unverified fields stop being Acceptable; export never blank.
    - **Phase 2: "not applicable" decided by the governing term.** Six fields get a declared rule. "Not stated" becomes "N/A · Physical make good" on 129 cells; base years are no longer fabricated and then deleted.
    - **Phase 3: ERP notes are notes, not figures.** No more Investigate manufactured by comparing prose to a number.
    - **Phase 4: the AI's powers narrowed.** Confirm, relabel with a citation, say whether a note matches. It cannot delete a value to make a field Acceptable.

    **What the client sees:** the same 28 fields per tenant; N/A fields read "N/A · reason" with the clause and count as OK by rule. Headline counts unchanged in shape. ≈110 empty Acceptables become Review/Investigate.

    **Weak point:** the client still reads 28 verdicts per tenant, some of them on fields we knew were irrelevant before we started. Carl: "we're presenting the end-user of the client with noise."
  </Card>
  <Card tone="accent" eyebrow="Direction B" heading="A hierarchy decides what is audited" badge="Carl's leaning" meta={["same four phases, phase 2 swapped", "not separately costed"]}>
    Instead of a rule that makes a field read N/A, a rule that decides whether the field is audited. The extra work is in the UI (grouped fields, "not audited" bucket) and the data store (a scope column); the saved work is the N/A kind and its verdict rows.

    - **Document coverage first.** No lease on file: one tenant-level finding, Investigate; nothing lease-sourced is audited. A named document missing: one document-level finding, Review; the fields only it carries are not audited.
    - **Then the gate per group.** Make good type, outgoings lease type, security type. Children that do not apply are not audited, shown collapsed with the clause. "Type not determined" is its own reviewable finding. Decided per lease.
    - **Then audit what is in scope exactly as A does.** Silent lease, "none", formula, not in force, ERP note, ERP empty: same reasons, same documents, same rule-based verdicts. ≈300 of the 674 cells are in this group.
    - **The ERP side of out-of-scope fields is still read.** A value where the lease says there should be none is raised on the gate field.

    **What the client sees:** fewer fields per tenant, grouped; a "not audited" bucket with reasons; no OK with nothing behind it. On 200 Marlow St roughly 720 fields audited and 485 OK instead of 812 and 561 (§6).

    **Weak points:** the gate can be wrong or move between runs, so child fields can appear and vanish; "out of scope" must not mean "not checked"; the rule that fields are independent of each other needs restating. All three are in §8.

## 8. What we need from this meeting

One main question, four open points it raises, and four product calls that stand whichever way it goes. The full ten design decisions are in the decisions write-up with worked examples, and I own them. The form at the end of this section is for recording what the room decides.

### The main question

<Card tone="accent" eyebrow="The main question" heading="Do we build A, every field with its reason, or B, a hierarchy that decides what is audited and then explains the rest?">
  Same complaint, same evidence, same first phase. They differ in what the client reads: A keeps 28 fields per tenant and makes the irrelevant ones explain themselves; B removes the irrelevant ones from the audit, with the same clause as the reason, and leaves fewer, grouped fields. B is Carl's current leaning and the likely long-term direction; A is the design as written, and B keeps most of it.

  > **Carl's words:** "once we know what type of make good clause exists, then we determine which other fields actually make sense to even [audit] in the first place … This may not be the best approach and I'm open to other alternatives. Again this is a discussion not a conclusion."
</Card>

### Four open points about B, for discussion

<Card tone="warning" eyebrow="Open point 1" heading="The gate can be wrong, and it can move. What happens to the fields beneath it?">
  The gate value can change for three reasons: the AI amends it after the engine has read it (it does so on condition standard today, and the type field is adjudicated); a re-extraction reads the clause differently; and, trivially, the order of a multi-value type can swap between runs (11 obligation-type rows across 204 buildings, order only, no verdict changed; a gate that reads the set of values does not move on this). Under B any real change in the gate makes child fields appear or vanish between runs.

  **Proposed answer:** scope follows the engine's gate value, never the AI's; "type not determined" is an explicit, reviewable finding rather than silence; scope is decided per lease, so one undocumented lease does not hide another's fields. And keep A's per-field reason inside B, so a reviewer can always see why a field was not audited.
</Card>

<Card tone="warning" eyebrow="Open point 2" heading="Out of scope is not the same as not checked. Does B still look at the ERP?">
  Two things can sit under an out-of-scope field. On the ERP side: 9 of 29 tenants on 200 Marlow St have a make good note on the payment amount (84 such cells across the three buildings), and one tenant-field holds an ERP figure for an amount the lease rules out. On the lease side: Larkspur Architects states a condition standard ("clean and tidy condition") under a payment-in-lieu / yield-up make good, which B's rule puts out of scope; Pingly and Pacific Commerce Bank do the same. If B simply stops auditing those fields, all of that disappears.

  **Proposed answer:** yes. Both sides of every out-of-scope field are still read. An ERP figure, or a lease value, for a field the gate rules out is raised on the gate field ("the ERP records a payment amount the lease says should not exist"; "the lease states a condition standard under a yield-up make good"), for a human to read; an ERP note is shown under the collapsed field for information. Whether the raised finding is Review or Investigate is open.
</Card>

<Card tone="warning" eyebrow="Open point 3" heading="Is a missing document a reason to shrink the list, or a finding for the client?">
  Both, but the finding comes first: a missing lease is Investigate at tenant level, a missing incentive deed or disclosure statement is Review at document level. And a missing document should not un-scope what the lease itself states. Carl's own example was the bank guarantee ("if we don't have a bank guarantee document, it doesn't make sense to try and audit the bank guarantee field"); I read it the other way: the lease sets the amount, the guarantee certificate is the instrument, so a missing certificate is a note, not a reason to stop auditing the amount. Worth settling in the room.

  **Proposed answer:** only fields whose _sole_ source is the missing document leave the audit; everything the lease states stays in.
</Card>

<Card tone="warning" eyebrow="Open point 4" heading="Fields were meant to be independent of each other. A hierarchy is a dependency. Does the rule change?">
  Today each field can be switched on or off per client and new fields can be generated in isolation. B ties a child to its gate: a client could not keep Make Good Payment Amount and drop Make Good Obligation Type. A honours the rule by reading the lease term rather than the sibling field; B can read the term the same way, but the product rule still changes.

  **Proposed answer:** restate the rule as "a group is switched on or off as a unit", and keep the calculations independent underneath.
</Card>

### Four product calls that stand under either direction

<Card tone="success" eyebrow="Product call 1" badge="recommend: yes" heading="Do we tell the client, before the first re-run, that about one in six of today's empty Acceptables will become Review or Investigate, and under B that their field counts will shrink?">
  These are the fields nothing was checked against: tenants with no lease on file, incentive deeds and disclosure statements we were never given, a formula the AI called a match. Under B they become findings and a "not audited" bucket rather than verdicts; the count of OKs falls either way.

  **Recommendation:** yes, and reply to Mara's last message at the same time. This is the fix she asked for, framed as such: every audited field now shows what was reviewed, and where nothing was, it says so.
</Card>

<Card tone="success" eyebrow="Product call 2" badge="recommend: review if still to start" heading="A lease that has not started yet, against an empty ERP: is that Acceptable or Review?">
  A new lease that starts after the audit date and that the ERP has not loaded yet. (Autohub's lease starts 1 February 2027; its ERP already holds that date, so it would read Acceptable with the dates shown.) And the mirror case: Hadley Rowe's $152,340.00 incentive was fully used before the audit date, and the ERP shows nothing. In scope under both directions.

  **Recommendation:** still to start against an empty ERP is <Badge tone="warning">review</Badge> (someone should load it); already used up or expired against an empty ERP is <Badge tone="success">acceptable</Badge>, with the clause and dates shown.
</Card>

<Card tone="success" eyebrow="Product call 3" badge="recommend: declared “not held”" heading="Fields the client's ERP never records: a finding on every tenant, or declared “not held”?">
  The client's ERP has no field for make good obligation type or condition standard. Today the lease side is flagged on every tenant, and our engineer has told Mara these are review-level for exactly that reason.

  **Recommendation:** let each client declare the fields their ERP does not hold. Those fields show the lease value alone, read "Not held in the ERP" on the ERP side, and leave the sign-off count. Everything else stays a real gap.
</Card>

<Card tone="success" eyebrow="Product call 4" badge="recommend: yes" heading="Do we commit about four weeks to the rebuild, with every change the client sees landing in the first release?">
  The first phase is the large one and is the same under A and B: everything the customer sees (reasons and documents on every empty audited field, the export, the no-lease finding, the new Review and Investigate verdicts) arrives together, and each building is re-run once. The A-or-B choice lands in the second phase.

  **Recommendation:** yes. A consequence: the engineer's make good fix and the explanations branch close without merging; their good ideas become phase 2 and phase 1.
</Card>
