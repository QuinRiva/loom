# What happens to a child's submitted outcome

When a child calls `mcp__t3-code__workstream_submit`, its report is written to disk and **one pure function decides, from the outcome string and the gate state, which of four roads the child takes** — the control plane then drives the consequences on its next pass. The four roads: **complete** (`done`, or a verdict that resolves a gate — the child is done and its dependents start), **route inside the review gate** (a reviewer's `needs_rework`, or anything a coder submits mid-round — the counterpart is resumed and you hear nothing), **yield** (an outcome that matches no route, an exhausted round cap, or a child that went quiet — the child is flagged `awaiting_orchestrator` and you get a steered card with its report), and **hold for a human** (`needs_human` — the child is flagged `needs_guidance` and you get a pause notice).

The sentence to carry: **only `done` and a resolving verdict finish a child; every other outcome leaves it unfinished, and the control plane tells you — a steered card for a yield or a hold, silence while a gate loops, and an FYI digest line only after something actually completed.**

<Callout tone="warning" title="The three surprises this document removes" focus>
  **A reviewer's plain `done` is not a verdict.** It completes the reviewer alone, dissolves the
  gate, and can leave a reworked coder stranded at no outcome with no flag and no wake (§2.3, §4).
  **A coder's `done` releases its dependents before the review** — the gate does not delay that;
  wire downstream work on the reviewer. **During a rework round you hear nothing** — the rework and
  re-verify messages go to the two gate parties, never to you; the first word you get is the
  resolution digest line or a yield.
</Callout>

  </Card>

    An idempotent recompute from durable state. It delivers the gate's rework and re-verify messages, kicks off released children, and composes every wake you receive — each under a deterministic id, so a wake lands exactly once.

  <Card heading="The flags" eyebrow="PIECE 4" badge="holds" tone="warning" meta={["awaiting_orchestrator", "needs_guidance", "awaiting_acceptance"]}>
    An attention flag is a *hold*: the child is paused, not finished. `awaiting_orchestrator` is the yield — you own the next move. The other two pull in a human. A hold clears only when a human writes to the child, its parent prompts it, or its outcome is set.
  </Card>

Two refusals sit in front of the table. A submit on a thread that is already `done` or `cancelled` is refused (except the one case of a coder its parent set done mid-round, whose loop-back to the reviewer is still honoured). And a submit whose decision would be `terminal` or `resolve` is refused while the child holds `awaiting_acceptance` or `needs_guidance` — completing would erase the hold and release the dependents the hold was meant to gate. The refusal text tells the child to submit again with a short non-`done` outcome, which yields with the flag standing. Note that the hold may have been raised by _you_ on the child, not only by the child itself; the refusal applies either way.

### 2.2 Plain completion — `done` — and how you hear about it

**Input:** a child outside any gate submits `done` (or omits the outcome). **What happens, all in one transaction:** the report is written to `<stateDir>/workstream-reports/<threadId>.md` (`~/.t3/userdata/workstream-reports/` on the live install), the child's outcome becomes `done`, any attention flag it held is cleared, and every sibling whose `blockedBy` names it is released — the next pass kicks those off. The child's tool result reads:

<Code
  language="text"
  wrap
  code={
    "Work submitted: report recorded, outcome done (dependents released).\nReport: /home/carl/.t3/userdata/workstream-reports/c4a9e0b2-researcher.md\n"
  }
/>

**Output to you:** nothing immediate. A completion is an _FYI item_, withheld and delivered as one digest per parent — as its own turn only when you are idle (`start_if_idle`), and only once your workstream is quiet (no child running or briefed-and-ready) or the oldest withheld item is two minutes old. If a steered wake (a yield, a hold, a brief-needed notice) goes to you first, the withheld items ride on it under _Also, FYI since you last heard_. The line you see:

<Code
  language="markdown"
  wrap
  code={
    "[T3 Workstream control plane — automated notice, not from the human]\n\nFYI digest — thread updates since you last heard. Nothing below is blocked on you.\n\n### ☑️ researcher `c4a9e0b2` — done\n_2026-10-13 10:02Z_ · released: coder `e71b0c55`\n\nReport reference: `/home/carl/.t3/userdata/workstream-reports/c4a9e0b2-researcher.md` (read the full report on demand)\n\nThe routing rules are in `explainers/workstream-outcome-routing/explainer.mdx`; three code-vs-prose discrepancies …\n\n_[excerpt truncated — read the full report via the reference above]_\n\nNo first-pass review is owed on gate-resolved items (their reviewers verified the work). Update your task tree / scoreboard, pull anything useful from the reports (follow-up work, findings worth acting on), and continue orchestrating. Unreviewed completions (marked ☑️) deserve the usual first look.\n"
  }
/>

The excerpt is the report's response block, relayed verbatim; a report without one (a synthesised one, or one written before the block was required) is cut at its first 400 characters, as here. The full report is the file. The ☑️ marks an _unreviewed_ completion — the closing line asks you for the usual first look, as against a ✅ gate resolution, which a reviewer already verified.

<Callout tone="info" title="Release is immediate; the digest is not">
  Dependents start on the next pass after the `done` commits — well before the digest tells you. If
  you want to see a completion before anything downstream moves, the dependency edge is the only
  brake; the digest is a notification, not a gate.
</Callout>

### 2.3 The review gate — one worked example, one rework round

The gate is two threads: a **coder** `e71b0c55` and a **reviewer** `a93d4f21` spawned with `gate: { rework: "e71b0c55" }`, which gives the reviewer two routes — `needs_rework` loops to the coder, `clean` / `fixed_inline` resolve — and adds the coder to the reviewer's `blockedBy`. A **downstream** child `5b8e2d07` is blocked on the reviewer. The round cap is the default, 2.

<Callout tone="risk" title="The stranded coder">
  Setting the reviewer `done` — on a cap-breach, or because the reviewer itself submitted plain
  `done` instead of a verdict — dissolves the gate but **never touches the coder's outcome**. A
  coder that was reopened for rework and has since routed its round back sits at outcome null with
  no flag: it is not a gate member any more (the reviewer is finished), not a quiescence candidate
  (its last turn ended with a submit), and no rail reports it. It shows in
  `mcp__t3-code__workstream_list` as in progress with no activity, and anything blocked on it never
  starts. When you finish a reviewer by hand, finish the coder by hand too. Only `clean` /
  `fixed_inline` finish both.
</Callout>

### 2.5 `needs_human` and the flags — holds, not labels

**Input:** a child submits `outcome: "needs_human"`. **What happens:** the report is written, the decision is `attention`, the outcome stays null, and `needs_guidance` is raised (the same flag a human stop from the board raises, and the one `mcp__t3-code__workstream_request_attention` raises for that reason). The child's tool result: _report recorded and needs_guidance raised — a human has been flagged; you are not done._ **Output to you:** a steered _attention_ notice — _Your Workstream sub-thread … is paused and needs attention … It has NOT finished — this is a pause notice, not a result_ — once per raise episode, plus the flag on the board for the human.

Two consequences worth holding. A child that raises `awaiting_acceptance` and then submits plain `done` in the same turn is refused — the 2026-08-21 audit the code cites found half of such raises erased seconds later by the raiser's own submit — so a child that wants to hand back _and_ keep the hold submits a short token like `blocked`, which yields. And your `mcp__t3-code__workstream_prompt` is a blunt instrument: it clears every flag the child holds when its turn starts, so prompting a `needs_guidance` child is you answering on the human's behalf.

### 2.6 Going quiet — the synthesised report

**Input:** a started child ends its turn (its last run completed) without calling submit. **What happens:** the quiescence rail considers it a candidate when, all at once, it has no outcome, no flag, no running or queued turn, no open question to the human, no unfinished children of its own, any earlier submit of its own predates the end of the previous run (so a child that submitted _from_ this turn is never synthesised over), and it has been quiet — measured from the later of the run's end and its record's last change — for the grace: **10 minutes** for a turn the control plane or you started, and **never, by default**, for a turn a human started (`quiescenceHumanGraceMs` is null unless set). A gate party parked for its counterpart is exempt.

### 2.7 How a wake reaches you — tiers, timing and the card

Every control message is built by one function (`controlMessage`) and carries a `controlPayload` the client renders as a collapsed card; the text the model reads and the card's items come from one traversal, so they cannot disagree. Two delivery tiers:

The pass that composes these runs on every outcome, route, flag and brief-set event, on every run ending, and on a 60-second tick. Each wake has a deterministic command id (`server:workstream-yield:<child>:<event>`, `server:workstream-attention:<child>:<reason>:<episode>`, `server:workstream-digest:<parent>:<hash>`), and a receipt makes it exactly-once: a yield is sent once per submit, an attention notice once per raise episode, a digest once per item set. "Already told" for FYI items is read back from the control messages stored on your thread, never from dispatcher memory, so a restart repeats nothing.

## 3. What this means for you

- **Read the first line of a card for the road.** _Yielded to you_ and _Went quiet_ mean the child is **not done** and owes you a move; _Needs attention_ means it is paused for a human; a _Gate resolved_ or _Completed_ digest line is the only wording that means finished.
- **Wire downstream work on the reviewer, never the coder alone.** The coder's round-0 `done` releases its dependents before any review; a rework round reopens the coder under them.
- **Silence during a gate is the gate working.** Rework and re-verify messages go to the two parties; expect your first word at resolution (digest) or on a breach (yield). If you must know mid-round, `mcp__t3-code__workstream_list` shows the round and flags.
- **When you finish a reviewer by hand, finish the coder by hand too.** A reviewer's `done` — yours or its own — dissolves the gate and leaves a routed-back coder at no outcome with no wake (§2.3). Tell reviewers in their brief that the only finishing outcomes are `clean` and `fixed_inline`.
- **Prompting clears every flag.** Prompt a `needs_guidance` or `awaiting_acceptance` child only when you hold the human's answer; otherwise leave it and let the branch wait.
- **Accepting a yield is `set_outcome done`.** That releases dependents and produces no further digest line (the yield card already told you); a yield is never auto-accepted.
- **A human-started turn never goes quiet by default.** If a human wrote into a child and it stops without submitting, no synthesised yield comes; you have to look.
