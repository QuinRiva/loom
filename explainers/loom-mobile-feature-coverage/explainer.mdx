# On your phone, Loom is a status board and a chat, not a cockpit

The phone app is upstream's mobile app with a thin Loom layer on top: about 620 changed lines in 19 files, against more than a hundred Loom-only files on web. <Sidenote>`git diff fa31b66bbb HEAD --stat -- apps/mobile` (fa31b66bbb is the upstream commit Loom last merged).</Sidenote> That layer covers three things: **the thread list tells you when a thread needs you**, **the chat tells you who wrote each message**, and **search finds everything**. Everything that steers a workstream (the workstream and goal panels, task trees, marking a child done, cost views, document comments) is web only.

Search across briefs, reports, goals, sub-threads and archived threads. Agent questions, with how long they have waited. Failing hub logins on the Usage page.

  </Card>
  <Card heading="You won't have" tone="risk" badge="web only">
    Workstream panel, goal tasks panel, goal create and rename. Sub-threads in the list. Live consult cards. Handoff receipts. Comments and queries on documents. Cost views, spend, the context-window panel, the usage meter. `/handoff` and `/retro`. Push alerts for Needs you or Accept?.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="warning" title="The one gap that changes how you use it">
A **Needs you** or **Accept?** flag never sends a push alert, and neither does an agent's question when the agent carries on without waiting for the answer (Loom's default). The phone shows the flag when you open the app; it won't come to you. Push alerts are upstream's, they need a T3 Connect sign-in, and they cover finished, failed, approval and blocking-question events only.
</Callout>

Everything the agents do on the server (spawning, routing, review gates, scheduled tasks) keeps running whichever client you use. Replying in a thread from the phone clears its Needs-you or Accept? flag, exactly as on web. <Sidenote>`apps/server/src/orchestration-v2/Orchestrator.loom.ts:350`: a human's turn or steer clears the thread's flags.</Sidenote>

How to read the tables below: **Full** means it works as on web, **Partly** says what is missing, **Missing** says what you see instead. In the Checked column, **code** means I traced it in the source and **inferred** means I reasoned from the code but did not run the app on a device. Upstream features both clients share (projects, chat, diffs, pull requests, terminal, snooze, scheduled tasks, side chats, thread links, upstream's daily usage chart, push alerts for upstream states) are not listed.

## 8. What this means for you

- **Use the phone to watch and to reply.** The thread list's flags plus the sub-thread count tell you which root to open; replying there clears its flag.
- **Open the app to find work; it won't come to you.** Flags and carried-past questions don't push. Check the list rather than waiting for a buzz.
- **Read plans and recaps on web.** On the phone, anything built from blocks (tables, cards, stats) is lost or garbled; plain prose is fine.
- **Accepting, closing out or restructuring a workstream** (mark done, stop, goal edits) has no button on the phone: say it to the orchestrator in chat, or wait for web.
