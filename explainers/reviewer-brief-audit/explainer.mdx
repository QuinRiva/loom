# Reviewer briefs since 27 Oct are shorter, not different

Since the 27 Oct change, orchestrators briefed **91 of 119 reviewers**: 80 gate reviewers and 11 standalone ones. **40 of the 91 carried at least one thing the reviewer could not have read elsewhere.** 10 of those 40 are standalone reviewers, whose brief is their only assignment. Read by hand, **7 of the 40 changed a verdict: 4 of the 80 gate briefs and 3 standalone ones.**

The other words are mostly the coder's brief retold (54%), the reviewer role, gate skill or AGENTS.md restated (12%), process instructions (10%) and the orchestrator's own checklist (9%). Before the change, the median brief was **370 words** with the same mix. The change trimmed what orchestrators write by about a fifth and did not change what they write.

<Callout focus title="What it means now">
Text alone has not stopped the habit. After the 27 Oct line, three in four gate reviewers were still briefed. The orchestrators' own reasoning shows they knew the gate would launch on its purpose and briefed it anyway: "even though they launch on purpose, since a brief still helps". Four other texts still invite a brief, among them the orchestrator role's rule that instruction text ships "through a reviewer gate briefed to veto it".

**Recommendation: refuse `workstream_brief` on a gate node and `brief` on a gated spawn in the control plane, and cut the four inviting texts in the same change.** From these 80 briefs it would have cost four verdicts:

- Two rework rounds a wider inline-fix licence saved. One sentence in the gate skill's fix licence recovers them.
- Two must-fixes the brief pushed up. The reviewer called one "at most one stray request in a rare case"; the other overrode the coder's own brief.

Standalone reviewers keep their brief, because it is their assignment.
</Callout>

## 1. What a post-27-Oct gate brief contains

Every word of every brief was sorted into one of these kinds. The brief and the coder's brief went to one model call each, with the reviewer role, gate skill, AGENTS.md and the project's rules. A second, stricter call then rechecked every piece flagged as new against the coder's brief, the coder's report, the purpose, and the role and rules. It moved 39 of 90 such pieces in gate briefs to restatement.

The permitted kinds add up to **6.8% of gate-brief words now, against 4.8% before**. The median gate brief carries **no** permitted words in either period. Three habits show up across the corpus:

- **51 of 80** gate briefs cite the coder's brief or report path, which the kickoff already gives the reviewer. Before the change: 105 of 217.
- **49 of 80** spell out what earns `needs_rework`.
- **55 of 80** mention inline fixes, restating or widening the gate skill's licence.

Across all 91 briefs, standalone ones included, the permitted kinds are 11.1% of words (9.4% before). That is because standalone reviewers have no coder's brief to restate.

<Details summary="Method and caveats">

- **Population.** `thread.workstream-created` events with `role` reviewer: 305 created 7 to 26 Oct (295 briefed) and 119 created 27 Oct to 4 Nov (91 briefed). "Briefed" means a brief file over 60 words. "Gate" means a `loop` route.
- **Judge.** `gemini-4-argon`, one call per brief, then a second call per brief that had flagged items. Word counts per kind are the model's estimates, scaled to each brief's real word count.
- **Hand-check.** I read all 15 post-change briefs the second pass said made or prevented a must-fix, against the round reports. 8 did not hold up, which leaves 7. In those 8 the finding was a plain bug or contradiction, the bar was already the role's, or the item only appeared in an "outside this diff" note.
- **Bias.** The model leans generous on "new". Its 30 of 80 is an upper bound, and on my reading several of the 30 are marginal ("Verify the claim yourself if it is cheap").
- **The rethink's study** counted per-change case lists as new content (its 19%). Here they are the orchestrator's checklist, because a reviewer derives them from the intent and the diff. Counting the same way gives about the same total: new content plus checklist is 16% before.
- **Files.** Scripts, raw outputs and the per-brief JSON are under `.artifacts/reviewer-brief-audit/` (`classify.py`, `strict.py`, `agg2.json`).

</Details>

The two gate briefs that saved a round share a cause. **The gate skill's fix licence is narrower than orchestrators want for documents and instruction text.** It allows typos and formatting, not a wrong figure in a recap or a cut to a model-read sentence. 55 of 80 gate briefs restate or widen that licence. The tool texts point the same way:

- The `workstream_spawn` description says to "lay out the shape with mcp__t3-code__workstream_scaffold and brief each node with mcp__t3-code__workstream_brief". It has no gate carve-out.
- The scaffold's `nodes` field says "A gate node's purpose is its brief until you replace it". That reads as a placeholder.
- One orchestrator read the scaffold text as permission to add to the purpose: "that still leaves room to spell out what the review should look for".

Citing the rule is not the same as following it. One orchestrator wrote "I want to add what it can't see in the hand-back: the stricter standard, meaning losslessness…". The brief it then wrote was, by both passes, entirely the coder's brief retold.<Sidenote>Tool texts: the workstream toolkit's `prose.ts` and `defs.ts`. Role: `roles/orchestrator.md`, the instruction-text bullet. Skill: the review-gate skill, "Instruction text under review". Reasoning quotes: `why.json` in the audit artefacts.</Sidenote>

**The 27 gates that ran on their purpose alone did their job.** 17 of them looped and all 27 resolved `clean` or `fixed_inline`. The sibling must-fix sweep judged **all 32** of their must-fixes as meeting the severity bar. For briefed gates in the same period it was 70 of 80, with 6 borderline and 4 not.

Their purposes already carry a bar when one is needed: "Hold PR A to the plan's §3.3: the ceiling only ever lowers authority, Carl's instance reads AGENT-OK unchanged…". This cannot show what those reviewers missed, but nothing in the round reports suggests they lacked their bearings.

## 6. Open edges

- **What purpose-only reviewers missed** is not measured. Only what they raised is. Comparing the next month's purpose-only and briefed gates on findings that surface later (after merge, or in Carl's own review) would settle it.
- **Cross-track facts.** The rethink's clearest decisive case was a migration-number collision with a parallel plan. Nothing like it changed a gate verdict in these nine days. Under a refusal it would have to fit in the purpose, which is a single sentence.
- **The two "saved a round" cases** assume the reviewer would have looped rather than fixed inline anyway. Reviewers often stretch the licence, so the real saving may be smaller.
