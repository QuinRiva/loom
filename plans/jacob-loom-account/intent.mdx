# Jacob works in his own Loom, with everything Carl's agents have, and his live threads follow him there

**Goal.** "Set up his loom account properly so that he can log in and use that, and provide him with the access that he needs to configure it as he desires … think about how we could potentially migrate some of his threads that he's actively working on to his account." His instance exists and runs (user `jacob`, port 13910, your release, refreshed after each deploy) but nobody can reach it, it has no model route, and its agents have none of your skills, extensions or GitHub identity, so he has used yours since August. Jacob adds: "a shared pool of account resourcing with cliproxy for all of carls accounts, and my own accounts"; "the full execution framework of skills and knowledge already provided in carls loom instance"; and "I like being in sync with the changes he makes to prompts/skills/extensions/loom, but also would like to have the ability to experiment, diverge from loom … or push to loom with a PR … i don't want to overwrite his pi-craft/pi-fathom, but should also be able to push PRs to them (in addition to potentially my own jacob-pi-loom/ repo for my bespoke things)".

**You'll know it works when.**

- Jacob opens `http://carl-dev.tail30bb77.ts.net:13910` from his own machine, pairs once, and lands in an inbox that holds only his threads.
- A thread he starts there runs a turn on `cliproxy/claude-…` exactly as one on your instance does, and its agents load the same global rules, skills, pi-craft extensions and loom extensions yours do.
- He adds a skill to his own `jacob-pi-loom` package and it loads in his next thread; your agents never see it. He opens a PR against pi-craft (or pi-fathom, or loom) from a branch in his own clone, and your runtime is unchanged until you merge. He can run his instance on his own loom build and come back to your release train without losing anything.
- An agent in his instance pushes a branch and opens a PR or issue under Jacob's GitHub identity, not yours, and **never merges**: GitHub itself refuses any merge to loom's main that is not yours, and every thread of his reads a HUMAN-ONLY shipping policy so his agents stop at the open PR.
- He logs one of his own Anthropic seats into the pool from his own Usage page, and both Usage pages show the whole pool and who is drawing on it.
- He edits his own units, settings and pi configuration by SSH as `jacob`; nothing of his needs you after the four one-off steps.
- **Migrating a thread** means: the whole workstream tree (rows, files, pi session, checkpoint refs, and the worktree's uncommitted changes) is copied into his instance, a verification pass proves it all arrived, and only then is the tree archived on your side. He opens it on 13910, asks "where were we?", and the agent answers from its memory. On your side it sits in Archive, one click from restored, until you confirm its deletion 30 days later.
- Your inbox stops mixing: the migrated trees sit in your Archive and, once Jacob says he is done on 13900, you revoke his login there.
- When his `jacob@unseen.id` seat's login lapses, he re-authenticates it himself from his Usage page; nothing of yours is needed.
- **Must not:** change your instance, database or units beyond that final archive; lose any row, file, session, checkpoint or uncommitted change of a migrated thread (the export bundle and a pre-import snapshot of his database are kept until you say otherwise); give Jacob `sudo`; restart or stop your server. The only things of yours that change, each additive and reversible: a second proxy client key, two lines in your backup script, one Serve entry, and a pi-craft path change that must resolve identically for you before and after (plan §2).
