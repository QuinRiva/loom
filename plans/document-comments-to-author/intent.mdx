# A document link takes you to its author's thread, and the parent hears once, when you say the review is done

Today a comment on a document goes to whichever thread you opened it from. In the five weeks to 11 Nov, **55 of the 101 review messages a person wrote went to a thread that had not written the document**, nearly always an orchestrator receiving comments on its planner's work.

The proposal has two parts. **Clicking a link to a document another thread wrote takes you to that thread: it fills the centre panel, and the document opens in the right panel.** You talk the document over with its author there. **The parent hears nothing while you do, and gets one hand-over when you say the review is done.** For a document its author is holding for your approval, that second part is already built. For a document whose author has already finished it is not, and it carries the one open choice: the question at the end.

<Callout tone="info" title="What the parent is told" focus>
  Nothing while you review, whether you ask questions or ask for changes. When you say the review is done, it gets one hand-over: the document as it now stands is approved, what changed, and your decisions in your words. A correction you type into the parent's chat is still passed on once.
</Callout>

**Goal.** "If clicking on that link defaulted to opening it within the author, wouldn't that simplify me responding to the author of that document?" Two things follow from your comments on the first draft.

- The author is the thread to talk to because it holds the context: "any questions or clarifications I have, they should be able to answer more readily". The orchestrator "has to go find out that information, which is a bit inefficient but also pollutes the context of the orchestrator".
- The conversation reaches the parent once, at the end: "I can have a bit of a back-and-forward conversation, wrap up the plan, finalise it, and then go, "Okay yeah, let's move on." That entire package gets fed back to the orchestrator."

**You'll know it works when.**

- An orchestrator tells you its planner's brief is ready and links it. You click the link. The centre panel shows the planner's thread and the brief opens in the right panel. You annotate two passages, answer the form and send. The planner replies in front of you.
- The orchestrator is still a tab above the centre panel, and one click puts you back in it. Its thread shows nothing new.
- The planner was holding that brief for your approval. After three rounds you write "approved". The planner completes, and the orchestrator gets the ordinary finished notice with a report that quotes what you changed.
- A planner finished its plan last week. You click the plan's link, go three rounds with the planner, and write "okay, we're done, let's move on". The planner tells you it has handed the plan over. The orchestrator gets one message: the plan is approved as it now stands, what changed, and your decisions in your words. It got nothing before that.
- You ask a finished planner two questions about its plan and change nothing. The orchestrator hears nothing, then or later.
- A link in a control-plane notice or in a child's report behaves the same way as a link in a message.
- You click a link to a document in the thread that wrote it. Nothing differs from today.
- You open a child's document from the file explorer or search, where nobody presented a link. It opens in the right panel of the thread you are in, and its header names the thread that wrote it with a button that takes you there.
- The author was deleted or archived, or the document carries no signature. It opens where you are, as today, and the header says your comments go to this thread.
- On the phone, in the orchestrator's chat, you tap the brief's link and tap two answers. The bar under the document reads "2 answers ready to send to Planner: intent brief", and its button opens the planner's chat with both in the message box. Back returns you to the orchestrator's chat.
- **Must not:**
  - send the parent anything about a review before you say it is done;
  - call a review done that you have not;
  - reopen a finished author, or hold up work that was waiting on it, because you reviewed its document;
  - make a document slower to appear after the click, including over the relay;
  - move or drop comments already waiting in a message box;
  - change where the Query button's question goes (it already asks a fork of the signer);
  - move you for a file that is not a signed document: code, a diff, a report.

The held column is the line in the sand you described, and it exists. In the build's own test a held author sent its parent nothing across the correction rounds in 10 of 10 runs. The finished column is the gap. You agreed on 10 Nov that a finished child tells its parent when a change is material. For a review of a document this brief replaces that with one hand-over on your word.

**Approaches in play.**

What the link does:

- **The link takes you to the author's thread in the centre panel, with the document in the right panel.** _Your pick, as you described it._ The cost: a link you only wanted to read moves you too, and the tab you came from is one click back.
- The document opens where you are and your comments wait in the author's message box, as a promoted answer from a query thread does today. Wins if you mostly open to read. Costs a second step on every review, and a comment can sit unsent in a message box you never opened.
- The document opens where you are and a Send button on it delivers your comments to the author. Wins if you want to fire off comments and not follow them. The reply arrives in a thread you are not looking at.
- The document opens where you are, as now, and names its author with a button to go there. Wins if being moved proves more annoying than the relay it removes. You have to notice every time, and not noticing is today's miss.

How a review of a finished author's document ends:

- **Your word ends it, and nothing new is added to the control plane.** _The pick._ The author holds everything until you say it is done, then hands over once. A review you walk away from is marked nowhere: the changes so far are in the document and in a dated note in the author's report, and the parent learns of them only if it reads one of those. Wins if you nearly always finish a review you start.
- **The same, and the author shows Awaiting acceptance again while the review is open.** From your first comment that asks for a change until your word, the finished author carries the same marker a held document does, on the thread and in the sidebar. It stays finished and holds up nothing. The parent is told twice: once that the document is under your review, once with the hand-over. Wins if half-finished reviews get lost, or a parent keeps building on a document you are part-way through changing. Costs a new state on a finished thread.
- A "Review done" button on the document, which sends your closing word for you. It can be added to either of the two above. Wins if the author misreads "okay" as the end, or misses it.
- Dropped: the author messages the parent whenever it judges a change material, as built. It is the piecemeal feed you ruled out.
- Dropped: the hand-over is sent by itself after a quiet period. It would call a review done that you had not.

**On the phone.** Since 11 Nov the phone draws these documents and lets you tap answers and verdicts, though not annotate a passage. A tapped answer joins the message box of the chat you opened the document from, so the phone sends answers to the wrong thread the same way web does. A document already fills the screen there, so on the phone the change is where your answers go and which chat the bar's button opens.

- _Assumed:_ the one hand-over replaces "tell the parent when a change is material" only for a review of a document. Other work you do in a finished child stays as the brief on talking inside a child thread has it. Redirect if you want the same line in the sand there.
- _Assumed:_ any plain closing word counts, such as "approved", "we're done" or "let's move on". The author says in its reply that it has handed over, so you can see it took your word as the end.
- _Assumed:_ this applies to documents an agent signed as its own (plans, intent briefs, recaps, explainers). 50 of the 55 messages were on signed documents. An unsigned document opens where you are, as today.
- _Assumed:_ you are moved only while the author thread exists and is not archived. An old plan whose planner was archived weeks ago opens where you are, with the author named.
- _Assumed:_ a busy author queues your message as it does when you type to it mid-turn.
- _Assumed:_ a thread that takes over another's document signs it and becomes its author from then on. In three of the eight cases where a second thread had changed the document, it was a second planner that revised without signing.
- _Assumed:_ the desktop app follows web, since it shows the same document panel.

**Not doing.** Telling the parent anything round by round. Sending your comments to anyone automatically. Comments on code and diffs. Workstream reports, which are not signed. Annotating a passage on the phone, which the phone does not offer. Moving comments already sent.
