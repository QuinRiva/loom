# What a thread needs back after it writes to the task tree

**Recommendation.** Stop echoing the whole open tree to unscoped threads (roots, `goal_continue` roots and unanchored children) and reply instead with **what you changed, what other threads changed since you last read the tree, and the goal's counts**. Anchored children keep today's branch echo, which is already small and complete. Add the open-items reminder at the two moments where forgetting actually happens: an anchored child's hand-back, and the root's notice that a child is done. Against what current code renders, on the last 60 days of writes, this cuts echo cost **81%** (from $373 to about $71, or $145 to $29 counting cache reads only), takes **every thread under 10% of its context** (23 are above it today), and keeps **1,233 of the 1,262** times a thread acted on an id only the echo gave it. What a thread loses is line-level sight of open work elsewhere in the goal on every write, which the evidence says was used **29 times in 60 days**. The bet: a write-time reply is the wrong place for "don't forget", because the echo visibly prompted a tick 3.7% of the time while 28% of anchored children still handed back with open items unmentioned.

**Goal.** Carl, on seeing the handoff: "I wouldn't have thought that this is *such* an issue, in general the whole task tree (particularly if limited to *just* the open tasks), is pretty small. There are a handful of threads that this can *really* grow quite large, so we do need to workout how to solve it - but I think we also need to consider the *value* of the task tree, and what purpose it serves both for the human and the agents." Fix the tail without taxing the typical case, and derive the reply from what each reader of the tree needs.

**You'll know it works when.**

- A root rewrites its tree and its next call spawns children anchored to the ids that rewrite created, without a `goal_task_list` in between (today's workflow, 639 anchors in 60 days).
- A root ticks a task while a child has added work under another phase, and the reply names that child's addition with its id; when nobody else wrote, the reply has no such section.
- The longest root sessions (the "Chain replay" explainer run, 686K context, 232K of it echoes) carry under 15K echo tokens on the same writes.
- A child hands back with its anchor still open, and the root's notice names the anchor and what is open beneath it.
- **Must not:** change what Carl sees in the Goal tasks tab; drop the ids a rewrite creates (a bare "Rewrote the task tree: 6 added" breaks the anchor-a-child workflow); make the reply a plausible rewrite source (every tree line in it stays bullet-less, so the rewrite parser rejects a paste); add an instruction where a fact serves.

## 2. Who pays today, and how often the echo earns its keep

**The typical thread is fine.** Median echo 735 tokens; median thread 896 echo tokens, 0.49% of its context. Anchored children are smaller still. Carl's instinct about the typical case is right.

**The cost sits in a tail of long roots.** The figures in this section are as shipped, which includes September's replies before done subtrees were pruned; the sheet re-rendered every reply with current code too, and the tail survives that (below). As shipped, 36 of 1,501 threads carry echoes above 10% of context, 10 above 20%, all roots; with current rendering, 23 are above 10%. The 21 threads with 50K or more echo tokens resident hold a third of the tokens and **two thirds of the dollars**, because every echo token in a 300-turn root is re-read on every later call. Roots pay the money ($441 of the $503 as shipped; $325 of the $373 current); unanchored children carry the biggest single replies but are short-lived, so they cost $47 ($33 current).

**The two moments a reply cannot reach.** The researcher's unmeasured suggestion, carried here as a candidate: name the open items in the child's branch when it hands back, and name the child's anchor task, with what is still open beneath it, in the root's "child done" notice. Both are facts the server already holds (the anchor and its subtree), rendered at the moment the evidence says forgetting happens. Cost, from the sheet's own counts: a hand-back carries 238 open sub-tasks across 253 children in 60 days, about one line each, read once by a thread that is finishing; the 350 child-done notices gain one line each plus those same open lines, which the root re-reads for the rest of its life, roughly 35K tokens across all roots in 60 days. Effect: not measurable before it ships; the baseline to beat is 28% at hand-back and 13 in 100 same-turn ticks. **It belongs in this change**, because it is what makes dropping the write-time tree safe to argue for, and because it is a dozen lines each on surfaces already composed by the server.

## 6. Rewriting from an elided echo deletes what it hid

Today's root echo hides finished subtrees ("… 12 done tasks elided") and ends with a footer saying it is not rewrite input. In 60 days, **80 root rewrites were made straight after an elided echo with no fresh `goal_task_list` between; 22 of them removed 212 tasks the echo had hidden**, and 109 of those were restored later from the Removed block. No rewrite ever pasted the footer or a marker line: the threads stripped them, or rebuilt the tree from memory of the echo. One lease-extraction root's rewrite reported "4 added, 1 edited, 1 moved, 115 removed"; 19 of the 115 were tasks the echo had elided, all restored afterwards.

September's plan named this risk and its fallback: "If it bites, the fallback is making the root's echo complete again". It bit. The brief proposes a different remedy, because a complete root echo would restore the cost this change removes (the tail's trees are 54 lines, two thirds of them done), and because the recommended reply **removes the tree-shaped echo altogether**, so there is no elided tree in the conversation to rewrite from, which is better than today; it does not touch the elided spawn snapshot, which 33 root rewrites started from. The fix, already a task under the build phase, is for a whole-tree rewrite to **keep a finished subtree whose finished parent line is retained but whose children are omitted**: the elided rendering then round-trips as a no-op, which is the invariant the September plan asked of every scoped view. The cost is that pruning finished sub-tasks under a finished parent needs the parent removed too, which the Removed block makes reversible. This is Carl's call in the questions below, because it changes what "a task you omit is deleted" means.

**Decided.** Carl's words, which the proposal keeps:

- "we want to ensure that the agent is aware of the items in the task tree, particularly the open items, so that it doesn't forget to do them. Echoing back, was a cheap way to ensure it was aware - but perhaps there are better approaches."
- "I thought we already resolved this? Didn't we make it so that by default, it only echo's back the *relevant* sections (so a sub-thread wouldn't see outside of it's branch)." It did, for anchored children; this brief reaches the unscoped threads that still see the whole open plan.
- "The *original* reason to do the echo, was that threads would often not handle the task tree correctly, because they weren't aware of it's current state - but we may need to re-think the approoach."
- September 2026 rulings kept: the anchor binding; **branch plus pulse as the bound child's echo** (the evidence gives no reason to reopen it: $15 of the $373, none above 10%, and it is the one reply that is a legal rewrite source); branch-scoped rewrite for bound children; `goal_task_add` legal anywhere with its placement echo; `goal_task_list` complete; the open-tree spawn snapshot for roots and the overview for unbound children. **Replaced:** the open-tree echo for the root (ruling 6) and for the unbound child (ruling 1's "open-tree rendering for unscoped cases", applied to echoes); both move to the slim reply.
- July 2026: "No just-in-time nudge" in mutation replies. Kept: the hand-back and child-done lines are facts on other surfaces, not a nudge in a write reply.
- The task-content contract (`plans/task-tree-content-contract/plan.mdx`) leaned on "the tree echo makes the accretion visible" to bound a ledger split across many short tasks. That guard goes with the echo; the 300-character cap, the register guidance and the root's routine full reads (94% of roots list) remain, and the sheet found no exact duplicate adds in 1,158.

_Assumed:_ unanchored children get the same reply as roots, since both are unscoped; the "others changed" section for a thread that has never read the tree is everything changed since its spawn snapshot, which is the cautious reading. Redirect if wrong.

**Not doing.** The spawn-time snapshot, `goal_task_list`, the per-turn injection and the human's panel are untouched. No short ids. No threshold on tree size unless chosen below.

## As built

Built on branch `t3/slim-goal-task-echo` in three commits (`fe3703c706`, `935154c84d`, `f168bd66ca`), each behind a reviewer gate that resolved clean. Where the build departed from the brief, and why:

- **The last-read mark is a column on `loom_thread_workstream` but not a field of the `LoomThreadWorkstream` contract.** That table is written only by the projector through a full-row upsert, so a contract field would be overwritten and shipped to clients; kept outside the row codec, the projector never touches it and no client sees it.
- **The mark is set at the thread's first launch, not at spawn.** That is when the goal context (the snapshot) is actually composed; a blocked child can be spawned long before it launches.
- **A task's `updated_at` now moves only on a change to text, done state or parent, or a restore.** `replaceTree` used to refresh every row's stamp, which would have made any rewrite read as "everything changed" to every other thread. Nothing else read that column.
- **The "changed by other threads" verbs are derived from current state** (`added`, `removed` with a count beneath, `ticked`, `edited`), since task rows keep no history; "renamed" cannot be told apart from "reopened" or "moved", and reorders are not listed. A thread with no mark (rows from before this change) sees nothing as changed on its first reply and is then marked, rather than receiving months of history.
- **The hand-back section appears only on submit routes that end in the parent's completion notice** (`terminal`, `resolve`, `loop`), not on `yield`, `attention` or `cap-breach`, where the header's claim that the parent's notice lists these lines would be false. The task line is also rendered for resolved gate pairs, so a gated coder's completion carries it; it reads `ticked` rather than `ticked by the child`.
- **The anchor is read with its own goal lookup in the dispatcher**, since the list view's anchor join lives in the MCP list handler the dispatcher does not use.
- **One fact correction the brief did not foresee:** `goal_tasks_rewrite`'s "a task you omit is deleted" now carries the exception for finished tasks beneath a kept finished line, and a rewrite whose only omissions were such tasks says so instead of "no changes".
