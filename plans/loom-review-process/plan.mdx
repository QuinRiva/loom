# Reviewers launch on their purpose; a brief is only for what the hand-back cannot show

Your question was why a reviewer needs a specific brief beyond "does this achieve goal X and meet the standards". Loom's own gates answer: **it mostly does not.** Of **262** gate reviewers since 1 October, **about 70% of brief words** restate the reviewer's role, the project posture or the coder's own brief, and only **7 gates (3%)** reached a verdict that depended on something only the brief said. Anthropic's reviewer gets no per-review brief at all: the diff, the author's stated intent and two standing files. Loom's reviewer already receives the equivalent at launch. So: a scaffolded review gate **launches on its purpose the moment its coder hands back**, no brief required; an orchestrator writes one only when it holds something the reviewer cannot read elsewhere; Anthropic's guidance is adopted where it is standing configuration and rejected where it is machinery for a large team.

<Callout tone="decision" title="What changes when you run a workstream" focus>
  A review gate no longer sits on **brief needed** after its coder finishes: it starts in the next dispatcher pass on the one-line purpose the orchestrator gave it at scaffold time (for example *"Confirm the deferral is correct on every path and cannot leave a node silently unbriefed forever"*). Orchestrators stop spending a turn on a reviewer brief that repeats the role. A brief still lands when the orchestrator holds something only it knows: a stricter bar for this change, a fact from a parallel track, a deliberate deferral. A ruling you give mid-coder goes **to the coder**; the reviewer reads it in the coder's session. The time saved is small (about a minute on one gate in ten); the gain is one fewer state in which a gate can never launch, and the end of restated briefs. Approve, amend or reject in the form at the end; two things there are genuinely open: whether to keep a way to read the coder's report before the reviewer runs, and whether to adopt two one-clause additions from Anthropic now or on evidence.
</Callout>

**In scope:** the reviewer's default assignment and where it lives; gates launching unbriefed and every surface that touches; the orchestrator's instruction about review briefs; a verdict on each Anthropic recommendation. **Not in scope:** changing the review gate's verdict routing, round cap or fix licence; rewriting `roles/reviewer.md` (the corpus shows no must-fix class it mis-names); any change for gates created with `workstream_spawn`, which already launch on their purpose. **Done** means a scaffolded coder-plus-gate pair runs end to end with no `workstream_brief` call, the kickoff the reviewer reads is the one in §2, and the orchestrator-role line ships through its own instruction-text gate.

The restated 16% traces to one line in the orchestrator's role, *"State the project's posture in review briefs"*; §2 replaces it. Whether the 19% changed anything is the real question, and the answer is: rarely, but when it did, decisively.

- **224 must-fix findings** across the 85 gates that looped. 36 of them (16%) trace to something only the brief supplied; the other 188 are plain bugs (57% of all must-fixes), intent mismatches (19%) or report claims the code contradicts (17%), all of which the role's *"verify, don't rubber-stamp"* and the coder's own brief produce without help.
- **78 of the 85 looped gates would have looped anyway.** In only 7 was every must-fix brief-only.
- **The brief that mattered carried one of three things.** A fact from outside the change (a parallel plan's migration number, real data rows to walk, a test asset). A bar stricter or looser than the default (*"a unique combination that identifies a tenant without naming it … counts"*; *"typecheck errors are not findings here"*). A decision made after the coder started (*"The planner left two questions open. The orchestrator has ruled…"*).
- **The third kind is misrouted.** In three planner gates the rework round was spent passing your rulings to the planner through the reviewer, because the planner was never told. That is a steer to the planner, not a review finding.

## 2. The reviewer's default assignment

<Callout tone="decision" title="Decision: the gate's purpose is its brief, and one sentence in the kickoff says so">
  No new default-brief template. A scaffolded gate's **purpose** (the one sentence the orchestrator already writes at scaffold time, averaging 25 words across the 259 scaffolded gates, e.g. *"Confirm the harness actually measures model variance (caches bypassed, tree frozen, replay control passes) and that the baseline numbers are computed as the plan defines them"*) becomes the text under "Your brief:". The role and the kickoff's gate lines already tell the reviewer what to verify and against what. One sentence is added to the gate lines so a one-line brief is read as complete rather than missing.
</Callout>

<Columns>
  <Column label="Chosen: the purpose, plus one kickoff sentence">
    The orchestrator writes the purpose anyway. It is already an assignment in its own words. The kickoff's gate lines are the one surface read exactly when a gate reviewer starts and never by anyone else, which is where Loom's prompt-authoring doctrine puts text that binds at one moment (`docs/architecture/prompt-surface-authoring.md`: control-plane messages are the only just-in-time surface). Cost: 27 words, read once per gate.
  </Column>
  <Column label="Rejected: a stored default-brief template">
    A fixed text ("verify the gated work against its brief and the project's standards") written into the brief file at scaffold time. It would restate the role in a second place, drift from it, and discard the purpose the orchestrator already wrote. Also rejected: a line in `roles/reviewer.md`, which every reviewer pays for on every turn, gated or not, and which the doctrine says must not carry "when you are in a gate…" conditions.
  </Column>
</Columns>

The one added sentence, in `gateClause` in `apps/server/src/loom/prompt/prose.ts`, after the hand-back line. **27 words.** It states a fact the reviewer cannot otherwise know (that a short brief is the whole brief), and it names where anything extra comes from:

  after={"You are inside a review gate: a `gate` names the coder <id> as the work you verify, and your mcp__t3-code__workstream_submit outcomes route it — your role's gate protocol applies from your FIRST submit.\nIts hand-back: report `…`; the brief it worked from `…` (later steers are not in it; they are in its session `…`).\nYour brief below may be only the gate's purpose; anything more in it is what the hand-back, your role and the project's rules do not say.\n"}
  wrap
/>

Under the four rejection reasons the instruction-text gate will apply: not *already covered* (nothing tells a reviewer a one-line brief is complete; its role says *"your spawn brief defines your assignment"*, which invites a consult when the brief looks thin); it *changes a decision* (whether to proceed or consult); not *derivable* (no tool output says it); it is a *fact*, not a rule. If the gate strikes it anyway, the mechanism in §3 still works; the sentence is a courtesy to the reviewer, not load-bearing.

**What the orchestrator is told instead.** The line that produced the restated briefs is in `roles/orchestrator.md`, line 38. The replacement names the three kinds of content the corpus showed to be decisive, and routes the misrouted kind:

<Diff
  filename="roles/orchestrator.md, line 38"
  before={"- Judge findings against concrete consequences and recovery cost, not reviewer satisfaction. State the project's posture in review briefs. Verify factual claims; defer optional hardening rather than adding machinery just to reach `clean`."}
  after={"- Judge findings against concrete consequences and recovery cost, not reviewer satisfaction. Brief a reviewer only with what it cannot read from the hand-back, its role or the project's rules: a stricter or looser bar for this change, a fact from outside it, what is deferred on purpose. A ruling made while the coder runs goes to the coder; the reviewer reads the coder's session. Verify factual claims; defer optional hardening rather than adding machinery just to reach `clean`."}
  wrap
/>

Removed 7 words, added 53, net **+46**. Each clause maps to a measured class: the three kinds are the three kinds in §1 (111 stricter-or-looser items, 106 outside facts, 19 deliberate deferrals in the 554 brief-only items the study counted); the ruling sentence is the three planner gates. "Blind spot from an earlier review" (13 items) is folded into "a fact from outside it". The instruction-text gate that ships this line should be briefed with exactly that: the file's purpose in one sentence and this +53/−7 count. It needs nothing else, since the four rejection reasons are already in the gate skill it reads.

The scaffold tool's own text changes with it, so the orchestrator hears the rule at the moment it scaffolds rather than only in its role: the `nodes` description in `apps/server/src/mcp/toolkits/workstream/defs.ts` gains *"A gate node's purpose is its brief until you replace it, so it launches when the thread it gates hands back"*, and the scaffold result in `handlers/scaffold.ts` says which nodes launch on their purpose and which still await a brief.

## 3. Gates launch when the coder hands back

<Callout tone="decision" title="Decision: the scaffold writes a gate node's purpose as its brief file, exactly as workstream_spawn already does">
  `workstream_spawn` has always used the purpose as the brief when none is given (`handlers/spawn.ts`: *"carries its kickoff brief from birth (brief, else its purpose)"*). `workstream_scaffold` is the odd one out: it creates every node with no brief, and 259 of the 277 gates since 1 October were scaffolded. The change gives a scaffolded **gate** node the spawn rule. Nothing else about how a node becomes launchable changes: the one start rule (`isEligibleToStart` in `packages/shared/src/workstreamStart.loom.ts`: not held, not finished, has a brief, dependencies done) stays as it is, because a gate now always has a brief.
</Callout>

<Columns>
  <Column label="Chosen: write the brief at scaffold time (gate nodes only)">
    Touches the scaffold handler, one optional contract field, and the line in the orchestrator that copies it. The start rule, the brief-needed notice, the web's "brief needed" badge, the kickoff composer and the dispatcher's promotion are untouched by construction. One invariant survives: a launchable node has a brief file. Non-gate scaffold nodes keep waiting for a brief, because a coder's purpose is a title, not a contract.
  </Column>
  <Column label="Rejected: change the start rule so a gate is eligible without a brief">
    The shared predicate would need the node's routes to know it is a gate, which widens its input type in the server, the dispatcher's two rails, the digest's quiet test and the web client's derived attention; promotion would read the purpose when the brief is missing. Eight files and their tests for the same observable behaviour, and the "unbriefed" state would then mean two different things for gates and coders.
  </Column>
</Columns>

**The reverse state: holding a gate to read the report first.** Today an orchestrator that leaves a gate unbriefed gets the coder's report piggybacked on the brief-needed notice, and 23 of 262 briefs were written in that window. This plan removes that window for scaffolded gates. An alternative considered, marking a gate `held` when a ruling is coming and releasing it with the brief, is **not recommended**: a held node is excluded from the brief-needed notice by design, and the coder's hand-back is withheld while its gate is unresolved, so a held gate whose coder has finished would wake nobody. Making it wake the orchestrator means a new rail for a case seen 7 times in 262 gates. The recommended reverse state is the one orchestrators already use: scaffold the coder without a gate, hear its completion as an ordinary digest item, then `workstream_spawn` a reviewer with `gate.rework` naming the done coder. The form at the end asks you to confirm this.

**Existing scaffolds when this ships.** Gates already scaffolded without a brief keep today's behaviour: the brief-needed notice fires (correctly, since the 26 October fixes on `main`) and the orchestrator briefs them. No migration, no backfill of brief files. Within a day there are none left.

**The saving, honestly.** On 23 gates in 262 the reviewer waited a median 1.1 minutes and one orchestrator turn; four waited 13 minutes to 4 hours, and no gate in the window stalled for want of a brief. The gain is not time. It is one fewer state in which a gate can never launch, roughly 75,000 fewer restated words a month across orchestrator turns (70% of the 108,000 written since 1 October), and a reviewer model that matches both your question and Anthropic's design.
