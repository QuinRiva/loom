# Pull 9 strategy — adopt Orchestration V2, re-express Loom on top

Loom is a Pi-first fork of `pingdotgg/t3code` whose product is an orchestration layer: goals, a shared task tree, and a workstream graph of durable threads with roles, briefs, dependencies, review gates, attention flags, plan lanes (a thread's position in the plan, `planned` through `done`, separate from whether it is running), liveness detection, isolated worktrees with fan-in, reports, and consult/notify. Upstream has now shipped **Orchestration V2** (commit `de34391427`, 1,914 files) with a native Pi adapter, and deleted the V1 engine Loom's layer is welded to. This document decides, area by area, whether upstream is heading where Loom is heading — and therefore whether Loom adopts upstream's mechanism and re-expresses its guarantees on it, or keeps its own — then rolls that up into one mechanism for pull 9.

**Done** means Carl has recorded a verdict on each of the twelve areas below, chosen the overall mechanism, and answered the open questions, so a follow-on planner can write the implementation plan for the chosen phases.

## The recommendation in one paragraph

**Re-platform, staged.** Take `upstream/main` as the base including V2 and the Pi V2 adapter, and re-express Loom's layer as a V2 extension: Loom's thread fields, commands and events as a sidecar contract on V2's store; Loom's decider rules as a delegation arm (redesigned for V2's per-thread locking, not ported) plus a bounded, enumerated set of marked hunks in upstream's decider and dispatch core; Loom's control-plane workers (dispatcher, fan-in, liveness, exhaustion resume) re-seamed onto V2's event stream and shell snapshot; Loom's agent tools moved from bespoke REST routes onto upstream's MCP bridge as a Loom toolkit, with upstream's `delegate_task` family **withheld** from Loom threads so there is one delegation model in every prompt. The evidence says upstream is heading to the same destination as Loom on the *substrate* — durable children as real threads, event-sourced commands with receipts, a durable outbox, restart reconciliation, a shared tool bridge, a separate fork migration ledger — and to a genuinely different (or absent) destination on the *workflow*: no plan axis, no dependencies, no gates, no goals, no human holds, no liveness, no per-child worktree. Adopting the substrate and retaining the workflow is therefore the right split. "Retain" is not a cheap alternative: V2 rewired the web, mobile and client-runtime around V2 contracts and gates `/ws` on protocol version 2, so keeping V1 means freezing the whole client and the store mobile app, i.e. a hard fork. The staging is a feature freeze on Loom's orchestration layer, production staying on pre-pull-9 Loom, a long-lived integration branch, and a cut-over gated by a real Pi workstream smoke test. Pull 8 (PR #323) should land first.

## What the evidence says, in five facts

The four research reports in `docs/upstream-sync/28-pull9-v2-alignment/` are facts-only; these are the five that carry the decision. Upstream code is cited at the reports' snapshot `a1d9d72aef`; upstream has since moved (eight commits by `7812230572`, five touching `orchestration-v2/` or `mcp/`, including `06e627448b` which rewrote `McpSessionRegistry` and `McpInvocationContext` — the code Area B hooks into), which is why the phase sketch carries a re-merge step rather than assuming a frozen target.

1. **V2 is an execution-fidelity model for one conversation, not a workflow engine.** Children share the parent's worktree; there are no dependencies, no staging, no cascade cancellation of app-owned children, no gates, no goals or tasks, no stall detection, no human holds; a role is a one-sentence prefix; the parent is woken with a generic "call `task_status`" pointer (`01-upstream-v2-inventory.md`, §A, §D, §E, §I and the closing *Design centre of gravity*).
2. **Loom is physically separated from V1 files but semantically welded to V1's vocabulary.** 24 commands, 25 events, a `goal` aggregate, about 35 projection columns, 45 migrations, about 20 snapshot-query methods, 8 in-place decider edits and 3 engine-invariant changes (`02-loom-inventory.md`, §0 "One structural fact"). The control-plane workers are portable modules on narrow seams.
3. **The Pi driver decision is downstream of the V2 decision.** Upstream's Pi adapter implements `ProviderAdapterV2`; Loom's implements the deleted V1 shape. If V2 lands, Loom's driver must be rewritten against V2 anyway, and upstream's adapter *is* that rewrite, fixture-tested on pi 1.0.0 (`03-pi-driver-comparison.md`, Headline 1).
4. **The merge is a merge plus a port, whatever the strategy.** 336 conflicted paths: 155 textual (pull-7 shaped, fits the pull-7 tooling) and 181 structural (95 modify/delete, 86 directory-rename relocations) with nothing on the other side to merge into; about 57,000 Loom lines sit on the deleted engine; 92 contract names used by 241 files vanish; about 125 files end up with dangling imports; `config.ts` silently auto-merges to `statev2.sqlite` (`04-trial-merge-surface.md`).
5. **Pull 8 barely shrank the problem** (348 to 336 conflicts) because it only removed the pre-V2 drift. The V2 surface is unchanged in size and the 110 upstream commits since the earlier dry run barely added to it.

## Area A — Thread graph and delegation

**Same destination? Partly.** Upstream and Loom agree that a delegated child is a *real app thread* with a transcript a human can open and message (V2 `creationSource:"mcp"` children take messages; `02-loom-inventory.md` §A intent), that its parentage is recorded, and that launching into a prepared worktree is a server workflow with progress and retry (`ThreadLaunchService`, `01-upstream-v2-inventory.md` §A "Held / staged creation"). They disagree on everything above that: V2 has no dependency or ordering between children, no held lane, no cascade cancellation of app-owned children, no per-child worktree for delegated tasks, and merge-back is context not code.

**Verdict — hybrid.** Adopt V2's `AppThread` + `lineage` (`parentThreadId`, `relationshipToParent:"subagent"`, `rootThreadId`) as the parentage record, and V2's `message.dispatch` as the kickoff primitive. Retain Loom's graph semantics as an extension: `blockedBy`, plan lane, `spawnGeneration`, `graphKey`, `isolation`, `fanInState`, `routes`, `anchorTaskId`, `kickoffBriefPath`, `reportPath` in a Loom sidecar record keyed by thread id (not as fields on `AppThread`: V2 threads are full-row upserts and `makeSubagentChildThread` spreads the parent into children, so fields there would leak into upstream's child and fork paths — upstream itself had to stop children inheriting PR links in `7812230572`); `thread.spawn` / `thread.scaffold` / `thread.dependencies.set` / `thread.plan-lane.set` / `thread.fanin.set` as Loom commands in the decider arm; the dispatcher's promotion rail and the fan-in reactor as Loom reactors on `streamDomainEvents`.

Two things follow that are easy to miss:

- **Loom children are not V2 "delegated tasks".** `delegated_task.request` requires a blocking parent run owned by the calling session (`parent_not_active`), publishes the first child run's output as the stable result, and never reopens. Those invariants contradict Loom's control-plane-created, dependency-gated, re-engageable children. So Loom never uses `delegate_task` or `OrchestrationV2Subagent` rows for workstream children; the Loom `thread.spawn` arm nests upstream's `thread.create` with lineage set (the command has no lineage fields today, so the arm sets them the way `dispatchDelegatedTaskRequest` does) and emits Loom events in the same transaction. V2 already uses this nesting pattern internally (`Orchestrator.ts:9884` "nested: dispatchDelegatedTaskRequest already writes child events").
- **V2's `subagent` lineage carries upstream behaviour Loom must accept or hunk, item by item.** Sidebar filtering (`filterSidebarV2VisibleThreads`) matches Loom's "children are invisible in the sidebar" — accept. Thread notifications are suppressed for subagent threads (`ThreadNotificationCoordinator.tsx`) — accept; Loom surfaces children through the attention rollup. Subagent threads are dropped from navigation shells (`client-runtime/src/state/threadShell.ts`) — accept; Loom's thread tabs and board are the navigation. Titles are reformatted for subagents (`models.ts`) — hunk, Loom titles are manual. The awareness relay is silenced for them (`AgentAwarenessRelay.ts`) — accept. Separately, `streamStoredEventsFrom(afterSequence)` offers replay that V1's `streamDomainEvents` lacked; the dispatcher's full idempotent pass stays (it is the right design) but its catch-up machinery shrinks.
- **V2 locks per thread; V1 was one serial fibre.** Loom's arm today writes to many threads in one transaction because V1 processed every command on a single queue. V2 dispatches under `threadDispatch.withLock(threadId)`, reads one thread's projection, and upstream warns the keyed executor is neither reentrant nor deadlock-aware (`Orchestrator.ts:9878–9894`); upstream only ever nests into a thread it has *just created*. So the cancel cascade (writes to descendants), gate traversal (writes to the coder from the reviewer's submit) and gate resolution (both parties `done`) cannot be "one transaction" on V2. The mechanism this document commits to: **the arm writes only to the commanded thread** (and to threads it creates in the same command), recording the *decision* as a Loom event there (`route-taken`, `cascade-requested`); **the dispatcher turns the decision into one idempotent per-thread command per affected thread** under that thread's own lock, with deterministic ids and receipts, re-driven on every pass until the read model shows it applied. Atomicity becomes eventual consistency with a window of one dispatcher pass; the rails already tolerate exactly that (they are idempotent passes over the full graph). An ordered multi-lock was rejected because upstream's executor gives no deadlock guarantee and Loom would be the only caller holding more than one key.

**What survives, and what does not simplify.** Survives: both start gates (deps `done` ∧ lane released) and the kickoff + `in_progress` write on the child's own thread; cancel cascade (the arm records the cascade on the cancelled thread; the dispatcher issues `run.interrupt` + lane `cancelled` per live descendant); subtree archive/delete cascade (hunks on V2's `thread.archive|unarchive|delete`, same pattern); worktree isolation with branch naming `ws/<parentBranch>/<role>-<id8>`, dirty-parent snapshot commit, and fan-in by `git merge --no-ff` with conflict → wake parent; worktree GC; honest per-turn diffs (start-of-turn baseline — attaches to V2's `checkpoint.captured` rather than the deleted `CheckpointReactor`). **Worktree provisioning keeps Loom's call site.** V2's `defer_start` only parks a run in `preparing` and records the strategy (`Orchestrator.ts:5158–5171`); the worktree and setup work run in `ThreadLaunchService`'s private `schedulePreparation`, reachable only through `launch` (a new top-level thread) or `retryPreparation`. A promoted child dispatched with `defer_start` would wait in `preparing` forever. So `WorktreeProvisioner` provisions at promotion as today and writes `branch`/`worktreePath` via `thread.metadata.update` before the kickoff dispatch; exposing upstream's preparation for an existing thread is a possible later hunk, not part of pull 9. `forkFrom` (acknowledge-then-fork) is decided in Area L.

**Users gain** lineage in upstream's panel and mobile sheets and provider-neutral children (any V2 adapter). **Users lose** cross-thread atomicity: a cascade or gate resolution lands over one dispatcher pass rather than in one write, which is observable on the board for a moment and recoverable by construction.

**Cost class — redesign for the attachment, port for the workers.** The fan-in reactor (1,026 lines), reaper (459), provisioner and shared libs (`workstreamDependencies.ts`, `workstreamGraph.ts`, `workstreamIsolation.ts`) move with logic intact. The decider arm and the dispatcher's write path are a **redesign**: the arm loses its multi-thread writes and the dispatcher gains the per-thread re-drive for cascade, gate traversal and resolution.

## Area B — Agent tool surface and transport

**Same destination? Yes.** Both servers write a T3-owned pi extension at session open, pass it with `--extension`, and give it a per-session bearer from the same `McpSessionRegistry` credential; both forward `pi.registerTool` calls to the server over HTTP (`03-pi-driver-comparison.md` Headline 2, §2). Loom's `T3_WORKSTREAM_ENDPOINT` is literally upstream's MCP endpoint minus `/mcp`. The difference is contract: upstream is a real MCP client with dynamic `tools/list` discovery and `mcp__t3-code__*` names; Loom is a static list of 23 hand-authored tools POSTing to bespoke `/provider-tools/*` REST routes with server-rendered text.

**Verdict — adopt upstream's transport, retain Loom's catalogue; withhold upstream's orchestration toolkit from Loom threads.** Loom's tool definitions (`providerToolDefs.ts`, 1,003 lines of description, snippet, guidelines, schema, `errorMode`) become a `WorkstreamToolkit` (and `GoalToolkit`) registered on `/mcp` beside upstream's ten toolkits, gated by the `workstream` capability Loom's driver already requests. The handlers (`WorkstreamSpawnHttp.ts` 4,061, `GoalTaskHttp.ts` 428, `GoalHandoffHttp.ts` 333, `ThreadForkHttp.ts`, `UserInputHttp.ts`) already talk to the engine only through `engine.dispatch` (14 sites) and five snapshot reads, so the port is at the edge: route → toolkit handler, `engine.dispatch` → `OrchestratorV2.dispatch`, `getShellSnapshot` → V2's `getShellSnapshot` joined with Loom's sidecar. Upstream's MCP tool `description` carries Loom's prose; the bridge's generic one-line guideline is replaced by the def's `promptSnippet` via a small hunk in `piT3McpExtensionSource.ts`.

The genuine collision is **`delegate_task` / `t3_thread_*` beside `workstream_spawn` / `notify_thread`**: both surfaces land in one prompt unless one is filtered. Resolution has two halves, because upstream's `tools/list` is **not** filtered by capability: every toolkit is registered unconditionally (`McpHttpServer.ts:715–730`), the capability check happens only at call time (`OrchestratorMcpService.requireCapability`), and the pi bridge registers everything it lists. (1) For Loom-launched sessions the credential is issued with `workstream` and **without** `orchestration` (one hunk in `McpSessionRegistry.issue`), so `delegate_task` — which would create a child sharing the parent's worktree, with no gate, no lane and no brief — **fails if called**. (2) Loom's own extension applies the role tool profile with `pi.setActiveTools` at `session_start`, and the profile omits upstream's orchestration and thread toolkits, so they are **not in the prompt** — this is what actually keeps them out, and it must run after the bridge's own `session_start` re-registration (an ordering to verify in phase 3a). The shared orchestration instructions that describe `delegate_task` are keyed on the `orchestration` capability by a small hunk in `piT3McpExtensionSource.ts`, so they are not appended either. Whether to later re-expose upstream's *read-only* thread tools (`t3_thread_read`, `t3_thread_list`, `t3_thread_search`) is an open question below.

**What Loom's extension still owns.** Loom keeps its own small extension loaded as a second `--extension` beside upstream's (both load cleanly per report 03): the role tool profile via `pi.setActiveTools` at `session_start` (select-not-restrict, so dormant families stay one `enable_toolset` away), `enable_toolset` itself, **`ask_user_question`** (a human answer can take hours; Loom's shim has a reconnecting long-poll, upstream's bridge `callTool` has none and Node fetch's default headers timeout is about five minutes — so this one tool stays on Loom's route), the prompt-debug sidecar, and the search guard folded in. The profile no longer travels as per-thread env (`T3_ACTIVE_TOOLS` has no carrier in upstream's per-instance env); the extension fetches it with the credential at `session_start`, which is more honest about "the server render is the single source of truth". The two extensions have disjoint responsibilities — bridge and permission gate versus profile and guard — which is not a dual-shape coexistence.

**Users gain** provider-neutral tools (a future non-Pi V2 adapter gets the workstream tools by issuing the capability), upstream's preview and PR toolkits in pi for free, one credential story and one env convention, and the retirement of about 1,200 lines of REST routing and bespoke transport. **Users lose** the bare tool names unless a small naming hunk keeps them (open question; default keep bare names, because roles, skills, memories and habits all name `workstream_submit`, and the `mcp__t3-code__` prefix is prompt noise).

**Cost class — port.**

## Area C — Lifecycle and state model

**Same destination? Partly.** V2's `Run` / `RunAttempt` / `ExecutionNode` / `ProviderSession` states and the shell's `activityRunStatus` are a more honest **runtime axis** than Loom's derived `executing / starting / idle / none` projection over V1 session + latest turn, and V2's settle/snooze/wake model is the one Loom already adopted in the sidebar rehome. V2 has **no plan axis**, **no attention axis**, and **nothing** that detects a running turn making no progress (`01-upstream-v2-inventory.md` §C "Stuck runs, idle and stall detection").

**Verdict — adopt the runtime axis; retain plan lane and attention as extension fields with their authority rules; retain the dispatcher rails, liveness and Loom's restart-recovery rules; two hunks in V2's dispatch/receipt core are certain, not conditional.**

- **Plan lane and attention.** The *plan lane* is Loom's record of where a thread sits in the plan — one of `planned → ready → in_progress → yielded → done | cancelled` — as distinct from whether its agent is actually running (the runtime axis) or whether a human is needed (the attention axis). It is the enum `workstream_set_lane` and `workstream_release` write, what the workstream board's columns show, and what dependencies test (`done` is the only lane that releases a dependent). *Attention* is the stored set `error | awaiting_acceptance | needs_guidance` plus the derived `awaiting_approval | awaiting_input | brief-needed`. Both become Loom fields and events in the decider arm with the single authority chokepoint intact: `in_progress`, `yielded` and `error` are control-plane-only (the `server:` command-id guard works unchanged on V2 command ids). Derived attention is recomputed at the read boundary from V2's `pendingRuntimeRequest` instead of V1 open-request rows — a simplification.
- **Is the six-value lane the right shape?** Only three of its values carry information nothing else does: `planned` (the hold), `done` (the releasing terminal — completion is cooperative self-report, so it must be recorded; V2 has no equivalent) and `cancelled`. `ready` is *not held ∧ never started*, `in_progress` is *started ∧ not terminal* — both derivable, stored today only so the board has columns, at the cost of the atomic kickoff write, the clear-all turn-start rule and the gate `reopen`. `yielded` ("parked for the orchestrator's decision") is an attention flag whose audience is an agent, misfiled as a plan position. The smaller shape is a `held` bit, an `outcome` of `done | cancelled | null`, and `yielded` in the attention set; the board derives its five columns from fields that cannot desync, and V2's thread projection makes "has started" a cheap `runs` check. (Rework would still have to clear the coder's `done` outcome, so the gate's reopen step shrinks rather than disappears.) The shape is a Loom contract field from phase 2, so it is decided now — but collapsing it touches the dispatcher, the arm, the board, the tool definitions, the role and skill text and the work-model addendum, which is a redesign inside a port during a feature freeze. The open question below therefore defaults to **keep the six values for pull 9 and collapse in a follow-up**, with the shape settled now so the follow-up is a rename, not a migration.
- **Turn-start rules** (§7 clear-all stored attention on any turn start; `ready`/`yielded` revert to `in_progress`; sticky terminal; started-dependents warning) become **one hunk cluster in V2's `message.dispatch` case** — the single place every run begins — replacing today's spread across `thread.turn.start`.
- **Human stop raises `needs_guidance`, orchestrator stop does not** → hunk in `run.interrupt` keyed on the command-id prefix, as today.
- **Idle-only wakes — a hunk in V2's dispatch core, not in a case.** Loom's `requireIdle` *defers* a wake to a busy parent **without writing any receipt**, so the deterministic `server:` wake id stays redeliverable and the dispatcher recomputes a fresh digest when the parent next goes idle (`OrchestrationEngine.ts:343–352`). V2's `message.dispatch` offers `start_immediately` (which also queues behind an active run, `Orchestrator.ts:4696–4700`), `queue_after_active` or `steer_active` — never "drop and let the caller retry" — and V2 receipts *every* outcome: a rejected command goes through `commitRejectedCommand` and a later dispatch with the same id gets `OrchestratorCommandPreviouslyRejectedError` (`:9710–9800`). So a `start_if_idle` implemented as a no-op or a rejection inside the `message.dispatch` case would receipt the wake and the parent would **never be woken** — a silent halt of the orchestrator that the phase-3 smoke test would not catch (its parent is idle when the child finishes). The correct shape: a Loom `dispatchMode:{type:"start_if_idle"}` whose busy-parent outcome is a distinguished *deferred* error that `dispatchWithReceiptEffect` lets through without `commitRejectedCommand`. That is a marked hunk at the dispatch/receipt boundary, and it is certain.
- **Zero-event success — the second certain hunk.** V2 rejects any command that produces no domain events (`"Command produced no domain events."`, `:9762`) and receipts the rejection. Loom's unchanged-value guards (a set-style command that changes nothing emits nothing but persists an *accepted* receipt — each redundant echo once cost a 1.5 s dispatcher pass, and one fan-in retry raised the same flag 13,420 times) would all become rejected tool calls. The hunk: for Loom command types, zero events is an accepted outcome with a receipt. Scoped to Loom's types so upstream's invariant is untouched for its own commands.
- **Settlement blockers** (`yielded`, any non-terminal descendant; finished root settles promptly; human send unsettles) → hunk in V2's `thread.auto-settle` case and `ThreadSettlementService`. Same shape as today.
- **Liveness sweep** (dead / waiting / stalled / spinning, 60 s cadence, one informed nudge then escalate) ports as-is; its heartbeat is written from the V2 adapter event stream (`provider-turn.updated`, `turn-item.updated`) into a Loom table instead of from V1 ingestion.

- **Restart recovery is Loom policy on V2's recovery service, and continuation must be switched on.** V2's `continueThreadsAfterServerUpdate` decodes to `false` by default and its continuation prompt is only "Continue where you left off." (`RestartContinuation.ts:22`). Loom's 28 marked hunks in `serverRuntimeStartup.ts` — a flagged, cancelled or approval-parked thread is not continued; a never-delivered kickoff is re-delivered verbatim rather than "continued"; stale-session reconciliation — become a Loom policy module on `ProviderRuntimeRecoveryService` / `RestartContinuation`, and the setting defaults to `true` for Loom (a settings hunk). Without this the no-silent-halt invariant does not survive a server restart.
- **The pending-steer stash stays.** V2 moved *queued* messages server-side, but a steer pi has already accepted lives in pi's process and dies with it; V2 does not track it. Loom's stash (`pendingSteering.ts`, 42 lines) rides the Loom continuation prompt as today, fed from the V2 adapter's steer path instead of V1 ingestion.

**What is verified before deletion.** Stuck-launch CAS repair (`stuckLaunchRecovery.ts`, 427 lines + decider case) and `thread.turn-start.fail` (40) addressed two V1 failures: a pi that hangs at spawn so the session sits in `starting` with no turn, and a `provider-turn.start` effect that fails before `turn.started` so the pending-turn row holds the idle gate forever. V2 has no watchdog for a run wedged in `starting` (nothing in `orchestration-v2/` detects it; upstream's `6108ef3d3d` fixes session-release record races and retries, not this), and under `start_if_idle` a wedged `starting` run counts as busy — which recreates the 16-hour gate stall the V1 code fixed. So the verification is concrete: kill pi at spawn and fail the start effect on a copied database under V2, and keep both modules unless the effect outbox's attempt retry provably recovers each case. Loom's exhaustion resume stays regardless (V2's `limitRecovery` covers provider usage limits, not Loom's per-account failover targets), re-anchored on V2's run error.

**Users gain** V2's run/attempt detail in the timeline and the settle/wake polish. **Users lose** nothing, provided the two dispatch-core hunks and the recovery policy land; without them the no-silent-halt invariant is not preserved.

**Cost class — redesign for the axes' hooks (new attachment points, same rules), port for the rails and sweep.**

## Area E — Goals and task tree

## Area K — Persistence

**Same destination? Yes.** Upstream's `legacy-orchestration-migration.md` now states what Loom's `22-migration-lane-split-plan.md` decided a year earlier: a fork's schema belongs in a separate migration table. Loom's `loom_sql_migrations` ledger at `1001+` is exactly that, and the trial merge confirmed the payoff — upstream's `Migrations.ts` conflicts on one trivial hunk, upstream's divergence warning and preview reconciler only scan `effect_sql_migrations`.

**Verdict — adopt V2's store, projections, outbox and `statev2.sqlite`; retain Loom's lane, re-targeted at Loom sidecar tables; Loom writes its own one-shot importer.** Loom's about 35 columns leave `projection_threads` (a V1 table that becomes dead) and land in `loom_thread_workstream` keyed by the V2 thread id, joined into the shell query — no `ALTER TABLE` on upstream's `orchestration_v2_projection_*` tables, so upstream's schema stays byte-identical. `projection_goals / goal_tasks / heartbeats / consults / peer_messages / usage_ledger` and the thread-search index move to `loom_*` names (or stay, if the migrator can rename cheaply). Loom's V1 event types stay in `orchestration_events` at `application_event_version = 1`, unread — Loom data is projection-table-driven under V2, not replayed.

**The dangerous step, in three parts.** `initializeV2Database` copies `state.sqlite` to `statev2.sqlite` once, so every Loom table arrives intact and inert; upstream's `LegacyV1ThreadImporter` reads a fixed column list and none of Loom's. A Loom import step runs after upstream's, reading the copied V1 Loom tables and writing `loom_*` rows for whatever scope Carl chooses (open question; default: all goals and task trees, plus workstream fields for non-archived threads only — finished workstreams stay as inert rows, queryable by SQL).

1. **Session identity must be imported, or every Loom thread forgets everything.** Upstream's importer deliberately does not translate provider session identity: an imported thread has no provider thread, and its first continuation opens a *fresh* pi session with at most a 32,000-character transcript handoff (`docs/internals/legacy-orchestration-migration.md`, "First continuation"). For Loom that is amnesia on every live orchestrator and child that is continued, notified, woken or re-engaged after cut-over, and `consult_thread`, `forkFrom`, `goal_continue` and relocation lose their target because there is no `nativeThreadRef` to resolve. V2 also requires a strong native ref before restart continuation applies. So the Loom importer, for every imported thread with a pi session on disk, creates the V2 provider-thread binding with `nativeThreadRef` = the existing `*_<threadId>.jsonl` path and `strength: "strong"` — `thread.create.importedNativeThread{ref:{driver, nativeId, strength}}` already exists for exactly this (`orchestrationV2.ts:2493`). The phase-4 gate **continues a pre-existing imported thread and checks that it remembers**.
2. **The receipt ledger is not migrated.** V1's `server:` receipts stay in V1's table; after import, Loom's deterministic ids for already-delivered episodes (terminal-child digests, gate legs, brief-needed rungs) have no V2 receipt. Left alone, the dispatcher's first pass would either re-fire a burst of stale digests at every live parent or never fire. Resolution: Loom data is projection-driven under V2 and imported threads carry **no Loom events**, so the episode-keyed rails (keyed by a Loom event id) have nothing to fire for them by construction; the importer additionally stamps imported terminal children as notified so the delta rail treats them as history. Suppress, not seed.
3. **The copy happens only when `statev2.sqlite` is absent** (`initializeV2Database.ts:26`). If production is rolled back to V1 after a cut-over and later re-cut, V2 resumes from the stale first snapshot and silently loses everything V1 wrote in between. Delete `statev2.sqlite` before any second cut-over; the runbook step says so explicitly.

About 50 tracked files name `state.sqlite` (scripts, the `AGENTS.md` test-data recipe, the `test-t3-app` skill) and must follow.

**Cost class — port plus one data step.**

## Area L — Provider registry and the Pi driver

**Same destination? Yes for the registry and the adapter contract; no for economics and operations.** Upstream's `ProviderDriver` SPI now requires an `orchestrationAdapter: ProviderAdapterV2Shape`, and `PiAdapterV2` + `PiRpc` (about 3,500 lines, 67 tests, 8 recorded live-pi transcripts through a replay harness) is the V2 Pi adapter already written. Loom's `PiDriver.ts` (2,959 lines, 152 tests including six that run the real patched pi) implements the deleted V1 shape and cannot plug into V2 (`03-pi-driver-comparison.md`, Headline 1 and the base-choice assessment).

**Verdict — adopt upstream's adapter; retain Loom's economics and operations as hunks and sidecar modules; resolve session identity in upstream's favour with a deterministic first file name.**

Adopted with the adapter: `agent_settled` + idle-probe turn settlement, extension dialogs → approvals and questions, native non-destructive rollback via `fork(entryId)`, native fork and fork-from-turn, `readThreadSnapshot` for handoffs, per-model thinking, live usage, the retry and compaction rows, the unsolicited-activity guard, `set_session_name`, `$skill` expansion.

Retained as Loom hunks or modules (sizes from report 03's list): bundled patched pi resolution and manual-only maintenance (`pi update --self` disabled); `--session-id <threadId>` at first launch; Loom open-session fields → argv (Area G); quota failover, tier-2 effective-model resolution, `model.rerouted`, the T3 slow-retry tier and `quota_exhausted` for the resume sweep — **re-anchored on `agent_settled`, not ported** (about 600 interleaved lines, the largest single item); cost and model attribution on usage (`costUsd` has no slot in V2 `tokenUsage` — a contract hunk); the codex → Anthropic tool-id sanitiser; account quota pollers; `PI_CACHE_RETENTION`; `queue_update` display. Stdout backpressure and `rawInput` merging are kept only if a measurement under V2 shows they are still needed.

**Session identity — the deepest collision.** Upstream's identity is the session file *path* (`nativeThreadRef`), resumed by RPC `switch_session`, rolled back by `fork(entryId)` which creates a new file; Loom's is a deterministic `*_<threadId>.jsonl`, resumed by patched `--session <file> --cwd`, rolled back by in-place truncation, and about ten Loom modules locate sessions by that name. Two identity schemes is dual-shape; the resolution is: the first launch passes `--session-id <threadId>` so the *initial* file is recognisable by name — and only the initial one, because every rollback, `new_session` or fork creates a new file, so the work-model addendum's "a file named `*_<threadId>.jsonl`" is rewritten to point at `$PI_SESSION_FILE` (pi-native, always current) alone — but **`nativeThreadRef` on the V2 provider thread is the authority**, and the ten consumers (`workstreamAsk`, `threadResolve`, `stallContext`, `workstreamLaunchIdentity`, the dispatcher, the fork and handoff handlers, the drafters) resolve the file through it. Rollback follows upstream (new file, ref updated). Patch 0001 is **extended to RPC `switch_session`** (pi's `switchSession` already accepts `cwdOverride`; the RPC handler just never passes it) so resume after a reaped worktree keeps working through upstream's resume path; the `PiCwdOverride` contract test is ported to that path.

**`forkFrom` (acknowledge-then-fork).** Upstream's `forkThread` forks natively in a throwaway process but does not replay launch identity, so the cache-prefix guarantee needs Loom's identity sidecar on top: retain `workstreamLaunchIdentity.ts` and replay argv in the Loom launch hunk when `forkFromThreadId` is set.

**Pi version.** Upstream recommends pi 1.0 and recorded fixtures on 1.0.0; Loom pins 0.99.2 with two patches. Both patches are still needed against 1.0.2 (`--cwd` absent, `auth.json` still written non-atomically). Default: bump to 1.0.2 and regenerate the patch bundle, because the adapter was written and tested against 1.0 behaviour.

**`PiSettings` collisions**: `enabled` default `false` upstream vs `true` in Loom (keep Loom's — Pi-first); `binaryPath:"pi"` resolves the global binary upstream vs the bundled patched copy in Loom (keep Loom's); `customModels` as objects upstream vs strings in Loom (adopt upstream's shape, migrate); the pi-only registry (one hunk, unchanged).

**Users gain** robustness (settlement, rollback, dialogs, fixtures) and the end of a 2,959-line driver Loom maintained alone. **Users lose** in-place rollback (fine — upstream's is non-destructive) and must accept a pi bump.

**Cost class — redesign-sized port.** The adapter is free; the re-anchoring of failover/retry and the identity re-pointing are the risk, and the latter fails *silently* (amnesia) when wrong, so it gets its own contract test.
