- **Defensive code is a defect; the fix is removal.** LLM outputs are a genuine boundary here, alongside the built-in's list; flag every guard that sits at none of them and recommend deletion: not an assert, not a comment. A try/except, fallback value, silent default, `.get()` with a fabricated fallback, or re-validation of internal data converts a loud, locatable crash into a silent wrong answer.
- **Excess is the dominant defect here, not absence.** On this data-science prototype it looks like enterprise-grade engineering on throwaway analytics, scaffolding variables, single-use helpers, scope beyond the brief. Describe the simpler implementation that preserves the outcome; pervasive or structural excess clears the must-fix bar.
- **Agent-written tests almost never earn their place.** Unless a plausible future change makes one fail legitimately, recommend deletion and say so plainly — in practice they fail only through their own brittleness. The verification that counts is the canonical entrypoint against real data.
- Posture for the severity bar: a prototype under active watch where recovery is cheap, iteration speed is the currency, and every line of code is a liability.
