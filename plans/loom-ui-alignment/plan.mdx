# Restyle Loom's surfaces in upstream's idiom, then run a two-model design council over the whole app

Six coder units restyle the three groups the audit found clearly off and sweep the nine slightly off. Each owns its own files, so all six run in parallel behind one reviewer gate. Then a new `design-council` skill in this repo runs Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5 over **14 named screens in dark and light**, three rounds at most, with one coder fixing Loom-surface findings between rounds. Three bets. Control cards take their colour from the **notice kind**, neutral unless Carl must act, derived in one Loom file so mobile stays untouched. The document renderer's hex palette is **re-pointed at upstream's status tokens**, violet folded into the brand blue, surface tint kept. The council scores **Loom and upstream elements separately on every screenshot**, so "within a point of upstream" is a number read off a table. Cost: about a working day of wall time for the restyle, then two to four hours of agent time per council round.

**What it means.** In scope: the web app's Loom-only surfaces and a reusable council skill. The surfaces are the control cards and the bubbles that carry kickoffs and steers, handoff receipts, the consult card, the staged card, the MDX document renderer's CSS, the workstream panel, the sidebar rollup badge, the goal panel's thread list, the graph, and a small-type sweep across the rest. Out of scope, as the brief says: changing what any surface shows or where it lives, mobile, upstream files beyond their existing `// loom:` hunks, a Loom theme. Done means three things. Every line of the brief's "you'll know it works when" passes on the seeded dev instance in both themes. The light-mode contrast check reports no metadata under 4.5:1. The council's final round has every Loom category within a point of upstream for both scorers, or three rounds have run. Four questions are open at the end: how a card that needs Carl shows it, whether the document renderer's own small caps labels join the sweep, whether the restyle ships as its own PR before the council, and one that is Carl's alone because it touches a Not-doing line: whether a large graph may open panned to its root so its labels are readable.

## 2. Control cards, bubbles, consult and handoff receipts

Three Loom files and the two `// loom:` hunks in one upstream file render everything that arrives from the control plane or another thread. Today their colour says which channel a message came from; after this section it says whether Carl has to do anything.

<Callout tone="decision" title="Severity comes from the notice kind, in controlMessages.ts only">
`controlCardModel` in `apps/web/src/loom/controlMessages.ts` already switches on `payload.kind` and `payload.notice` to pick a label. It gains one more derived field, `severity: "neutral" | "warning" | "destructive"`, from the table below, and `CHANNEL_CLASSES` (six Tailwind strings per channel) is replaced by `CHANNEL_ICONS` (an icon and a word per channel) plus `SEVERITY_CLASSES` (a border and a text colour per severity). The alternative, a `severity` field on the wire payload, would be the right long-term home but touches `packages/contracts`, the server emitters and mobile for a purely visual call, and the brief says mobile is untouched.

**Steps, in order, for the unit "Control cards and bubbles"** (files: `controlMessages.ts`, `ControlDigestCard.tsx`, `ConsultCardRow.tsx`, the `// loom:` hunks of `components/chat/TimelineUserMessageRow.tsx`; `controlMessages.test.ts` changes only if removing `CHANNEL_CLASSES` breaks an import there):

1. **Model.** In `controlMessages.ts`, add `severity` to the `card` and `raw` members of `ControlCardModel`, a `NOTICE_SEVERITY: Record<LoomControlNoticeKind, Severity>` table matching the rows above, and `channel` kept as is. Replace `CHANNEL_CLASSES` with `CHANNEL_ICONS: Record<ControlChannel, { icon: LucideIcon; word: string }>` (`InboxIcon` and "Control plane"; `MessagesSquareIcon` and "Thread") and `SEVERITY_CLASSES: Record<Severity, { border: string; text: string }>` (`border-border/80` and `text-muted-foreground`; `border-warning/50` and `text-warning-foreground`; `border-destructive/50` and `text-destructive-foreground`). No test for the table: `Record<LoomControlNoticeKind, Severity>` makes the compiler reject a missing kind, and a case per row would only restate the row.
2. **Card frame.** In `ControlDigestCard.tsx`, the outer `div` becomes `rounded-2xl border bg-card/70` plus `SEVERITY_CLASSES[severity].border`, the frame `ProposedPlanCard` uses. The header button mirrors upstream's `FoldedMessageHeader` order (icon, label word, summary, chevron) at `text-xs`: the channel icon at `size-3.5` and the label (`Digest`, `Rework round`) both in the severity text colour, the summary in `text-foreground`, the chevron `text-muted-foreground`. The kicker's `tracking-wide uppercase` goes. The synthesised marker becomes `<Badge size="sm" variant="warning">`.
3. **Items.** `item.role` renders as a plain word, `font-mono text-xs text-muted-foreground`, before the sender; the bordered chip and `classes.chip` go. The kind label keeps the lower-case wording `ITEM_KIND_LABELS` already holds (`finished`, `gate resolved`) and, with the status, becomes `text-xs text-muted-foreground` with the CSS `uppercase` removed; sender stays `font-medium text-foreground`; dividers become `border-border/60`; the `text-foreground/82`, `/75` and `text-muted-foreground/60`, `/70`, `/80` dims become the plain tokens. "Open report" stays hover-revealed but in `text-xs text-muted-foreground`.
4. **Document chips.** `ReportDocumentChips` drops its hand-rolled `rounded border font-mono text-3xs` class and `chipClass` prop for `<Badge variant="outline" render={<button type="button" />}>` on an openable document, `<Badge variant="outline">` struck through and inert on a missing one, and the "+n more" chip as `<InlineButton tone="muted">`. The existence check, tooltip and open behaviour do not change.
5. **Raw payload toggle.** "Show raw payload" becomes `<InlineButton tone="muted">` in sentence case at `text-xs`; the `pre` keeps `font-mono text-2xs` (a code surface, not metadata).
6. **Bubbles.** In `TimelineUserMessageRow.tsx`, the loom hunk that adds `CHANNEL_CLASSES[channel].bubble` to the user bubble is removed, so a kickoff brief or orchestrator steer sits on the neutral `bg-message` surface like any message. The loom hunk that draws the uppercase kicker inside the bubble moves to the slot upstream uses for `Sent by T3 Code` (the `p` with `data-user-message-attribution`), as the channel icon plus `loomBubble.label` in `text-2xs text-muted-foreground`, no alpha. `MessageAuthorHeading` keeps the label for screen readers as it does today. Every hunk keeps its `// loom:` mark.
7. **Consult card.** In `ConsultCardRow.tsx`, the frame `rounded-lg border border-info/25 bg-info/6` becomes the same neutral `rounded-2xl border border-border/80 bg-card/70`; `hover:bg-info/10` becomes `hover:bg-foreground/[0.06]`; the question `blockquote` border `border-info/40` becomes `border-border`; the `STATUS` words lose `tracking-wide uppercase` and their `/80` alpha, keeping their tones (waiting warning, answered info, failed destructive) since those are states. The `MessageCircleQuestionMarkIcon` stays `text-info-foreground` as the one mark of the consult channel, mirroring the control cards' icon-plus-word.

In `LoomTimelineRowView.tsx`, the `STATE` table's `box` strings and the `border-l-2` stripe go. <Sidenote>Today: `border-l-info/65 bg-info/5`, `border-l-warning bg-warning/8`, `border-l-success/55 bg-success/5`.</Sidenote> The receipt is `rounded-2xl border border-border/80 bg-card/70 px-3 py-2 text-xs`, the `FoldedMessageHeader` footprint. ### 8.6 How "within a point of upstream" is measured

Per scorer, per category: the Loom score is at least the upstream score minus one. The brief's check passes in a round when all fourteen cells (two scorers × seven categories) hold. The orchestrator reports the cells that fail by name ("Opus 5.5, Colour and contrast: upstream 9, Loom 7"), and the coder's build list for the next round leads with the must-fix findings behind those cells.

### 8.7 The lockstep check

After round one, the two scorers move in lockstep when their Loom columns differ by less than one in every category **and** at least four of five of their must-fix findings name the same screen and element. Then round two adds `round-2/calibration/`: two public product screenshots of the Codex desktop app. The capture thread fetches them from OpenAI's product pages and records the URLs in the manifest, and the scorer brief tells each scorer to score the calibration set first on the same rubric, before the app. If the scorers disagree in round one, a spread of one or more in at least two categories, no calibration is added. The brief's unknown is then settled the other way: two models from one provider are a useful check on each other here.

### 8.8 The stop rule

Stop after round n when any of these holds, in this order:

1. The within-a-point check holds and the build list is empty.
2. n = 3.
3. No Loom category improved on round n − 1 and at least one fell: the loop has stopped paying, and the coder's last round is kept or reverted on the orchestrator's judgement of the findings, said in the PR.

And Theo's line, taken into the skill: the orchestrator fights scope creep. A finding that would change what a surface shows or where it lives is deferred, not built, however good it is.

**The contrast check.** A snippet run from the browser tool's page-evaluate on each light-mode screen. On the `/preview` page it visits every element with its own text node under each Loom fixture group's root (control cards, timeline rows, consult card, goal tasks panel, Workstream, Subscription meter, Context viz, MDX plan document), and on the dev instance under `[data-control-card]`, `[data-consult-card]`, `[data-loom-handoff]`, `[data-loom-promotion]`, `[data-loom-rollup]`, the Workstream panel and the goal panel. For each it takes the computed `color`, or `fill` for SVG `text` (the graph and the charts), walks ancestors to the first non-transparent `background-color` and composites any alpha over it, computes WCAG relative luminance for both, and prints every element whose ratio `(L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05)` is under 4.5, with its text. The snippet is pasted, not committed. The capture thread records the lowest ratio per surface in its report beside the audit's 2.7:1. By construction the new values pass: light `--muted-foreground`, zinc-500, on white is about 4.8:1, and dark `--secondary-label`, #8f8f8f, on black about 6.6:1. The check exists to catch a dim that survived the sweep.
