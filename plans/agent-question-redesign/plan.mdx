# Agent → human questions a cold reader can answer

**Objective.** When an agent in Loom puts a question to Carl, he is usually arriving from another thread, hours later, having read none of the agent's transcript, none of its children's reports and none of the plans it has open. Today only ≈28% of the 74 real question sets ever asked were answered by simply picking an option; 26% drew an explicit *"I don't understand / I have no context"*, and 34% were never answered through the panel at all (median 7.5 h open). The two research reports this plan builds on — the failure diagnosis at `/home/Carl/.t3/cockpit/userdata/workstream-reports/ec443a94-30fc-4826-84b1-8eff824119ce.md` and the archaeology of the retired Loom question card at `/home/Carl/.t3/cockpit/userdata/workstream-reports/c514bac4-f2a5-45a0-9053-7b834771b619.md` — establish *why*: the failures are **vocabulary, not wording**. Agents name things with labels they coined (`D7`, `a1/a2`, `must-fix #1`, `§7`), lean on artefacts the reader never opened, gloss nothing, give no example, and bundle several coupled decisions into a panel that shows one at a time. And the riskiest moment is system-detectable: a turn woken by a child's report that ends in a question (22 of 74 sets, ~70% failed or unanswered).

**Done means:** a question asked by any Loom agent can be answered by Carl from the question surface alone — no transcript, no report — or it is not asked as a panel question at all but as a reviewable MDX document with the evidence beside each decision, which the panel then points at. The Q80 / AIT-35 set below is the acceptance case: under this plan it is a one-question pointer at a walked-through document, not three dense questions about "the processed blob", "the DI grid cell" and "stale column ids".

This is a plan for Carl to review, not an implementation. It takes a position on each of the seven dimensions the brief names; the genuinely balanced calls are collected in the single form at the bottom.

## The diagnosis in one paragraph, and what it rules out

Both reports agree on three things this plan treats as constraints. First, the tool text *already* says "the user has none of your context" (`apps/server/src/provider/Drivers/Pi/providerToolDefs.ts` guideline 4) — it just turns that premise into two instructions about form (state stakes, write tradeoffs) and none about vocabulary. Second, **structured fields did not help**: `stakes` and `recommended` existed from 27 July to 21 September and the comprehension-failure rate was ≈30% before and ≈25% during. A redesign that only adds prose slots will repeat that result, so this plan adds *no* new prose field. Third, no version of the tool or card ever carried context or rendered anything but plain text, so "resurrect the old card" would not fix the cold-read problem, and Carl's ruling when it was retired stands: build on upstream's panel, add only what is missed.

What *does* have evidence behind it: Q70 (defines the thing in the question) and Q74 (quotes the proposed text, asked while the report was open) were answered cleanly; Q43 → Q44 is a natural experiment — the same decision asked cold drew *"I don't understand this second issue"* and re-asked after a prose explanation drew "Fix it" in four minutes; and AIT-35 itself recovered the moment the agent switched to an MDX walkthrough. The house already owns the right surface (`mdx-visual-recap`'s decision skeleton: evidence beside each decision, `ReviewChoice` / `QuestionForm`) and the right rule (`roles/orchestrator.md`: "communicate through artefacts, not chat walls"; "assume the human has NOT read any child's report"). Nothing connects them to `ask_user_question`. This plan is mostly that connection, plus the smallest set of panel changes that let a cold reader see what they are being asked and when.

**Where the rule lives so agents follow it:** in the tool text itself (a guideline that names the artefact route and the `mdx-visual-recap` skeleton by name — the tool is the thing the agent is looking at when it decides to ask), restated in `roles/orchestrator.md` where the "artefacts, not chat walls" bullet already lives, and delivered once more at the moment of highest risk by the wake notice (below). **Does the tool need to point at an artefact?** Yes — but not with a new field. ## Dimension 1 — the contract a question must satisfy for a cold reader

A panel question's body reads, in this order, for someone who has read nothing else:

1. **Situation** — one sentence: what you are working on and what just happened (the ticket or feature by the name the human knows, the person or event that triggered this).
2. **The concrete thing, with one example** — what the reader would *see* today: a named file, record, building, screen or cell, and its literal content. Plain words for every term the first time it appears; if a term cannot be glossed in half a sentence, it is the wrong term.
3. **Why you cannot decide it yourself** — the fork is a preference, irreversible, or destructive; say which.
4. **Your recommendation**, in words, so that "dismiss" has a defined meaning.
5. Then the options, each a plain-words *outcome* ("one shared source link per cover list now"), never a code ("a1"), with its tradeoff as the description.

It must **not** contain: a label you or another agent coined (`D7`, `a1/a2`, `option (c)`, `must-fix #1`, `§6.3`, `Phase 2`, `Miss 3`, a task id); a reference to a report, plan, review or earlier message the reader has not opened ("the report warns", "per the reviewer's third point", "your open M1 question"); an unglossed identifier, acronym or code symbol; a request for approval of something you could simply do and report.

**Enforcement, ranked honestly by what changes model behaviour.** The evidence says positive directives about form are inert (the `stakes`/`recommended` era). Three levers remain, and this plan uses all three, cheapest first:

- **Negative constraints in the tool text, naming the concrete failures.** Carl's own pi-craft ablation found that negative constraints and specific-failure-mode rules are the ones that earn their tokens on frontier models. Guideline 4 today has zero of them; the rewrite below is mostly them. This is the weakest lever on its own and the cheapest; it is also where the *routing* rule must live, and routing removes the hardest cases from the panel altogether.
- **A narrow server-side lint that refuses, not asks.** `validateAskUserQuestions` in `apps/server/src/mcp/UserInputHttp.ts` already 400s on an empty header or a reserved label, and a 400's `message` reaches the model as a thrown tool error it can act on (`providerToolExtension.ts` `call`, `errorMode: "throw"`). Add one regex over header, body, option labels and descriptions for the *detectable* subset of coined codes — bare `D7`/`F1`/`M1`-style codes, `a1`/`b2`, `§n`, `must-fix #n`, `Miss n`, `option (c)` — returning "questions[0] names something with a label the reader has never seen ('D7'); say what it is in plain words." It cannot catch jargon or a missing example; it *can* catch the pattern behind 17 of the 74 sets (M1), and unlike text it cannot be skimmed past. About ten lines; reversible in one.
- **Placing the instruction at the moment of failure.** Ambient role text ("assume the human has NOT read any child's report") demonstrably did not fire when a report arrived. One sentence appended to the wake notice that delivers a child's report does fire, because it is in the same message as the thing about to be relayed. See dimension 3.

What this plan deliberately does **not** do: add `stakes` back as a field (the body carries it — the evidence is that the slot did not help), add a `context` field (a slot filled with the same vocabulary fails the same way; the body *is* the context), or relax `minItems: 2` on options. A question with no two defensible options is not a panel question — it is the "explain first" case, and the routing rule sends it to a document. Relaxing the schema would legitimise exactly the bare "help me understand this" panel question the corpus shows failing.

## Dimension 3 — the high-risk moment

Of the 22 sets asked in a turn that a control-plane notice had just woken, roughly 70% failed or went unanswered. The system knows this moment: a child's terminal report reaches the parent through the digest rails in `apps/server/src/orchestration/Layers/WorkstreamDispatcher.ts` (`buildStandaloneDigest` / `buildDigestPiggyback`, closing with `DIGEST_CLOSING`), and the 600-character report excerpt those rails inline (`WAKE_REPORT_EXCERPT_LIMIT`) is precisely the vector by which a child's `D7` lands in the parent's next sentence to the human.

Three options were weighed. **Refuse** bare panel questions in a notice-woken turn: strongest, but it needs the turn's trigger plumbed from the dispatcher to the HTTP broker, it blocks legitimate quick questions, and the lint already refuses the code-leak residue state-independently. **Require a briefing preamble** as a schema field: a slot, and slots did not help. **Inject one sentence into the notice**: the instruction arrives in the same message as the report it guards against, costs ~40 tokens per wake, touches one constant, and is the lever that ambient role text lacked. ## Dimension 4 — bundling

Guideline 3 today says "put every related clarification into that one call (up to four)"; the panel shows one question at a time and auto-advances. The corpus's bundles: 12 sets with three or more questions, of which 3 were clean, 4 confused, 2 unanswered, 1 half-answered. In Q5 question 3 depended on question 1; in Q80 all three were one regression.

Position: **fewer questions, and make the set visible when there is one.** The rewritten guidelines 5 flip the default to one question, two if independent, and send coupled sets to a document. The schema keeps `maxItems: 4` (upstream parity, Q74). On the panel, when a set has more than one question, a one-line strip of every header with its answered/unanswered state sits above the active question, so a reader sees the whole set before answering the first — the accordion-lite the retired card had, at a fraction of its size. A server cap at two was considered and rejected: it would break the one bundling pattern that demonstrably works (quoted-text questions while the reader has the document open) to prevent a pattern the guideline and the routing already remove.

**Increment 2 — the panel a cold reader needs.** Markdown body with file chips (the pointer becomes one click), age, the `recommended` badge (contract hunk, Slack lights up), the header strip for multi-question sets, no auto-submit, and the reply-in-chat control that restores supersede on web. ## Risks

- **Prompt text is the weakest lever, and increment 1 leans on it.** Mitigations are structural: the lint refuses the detectable residue; routing moves the hardest cases off the panel; the digest sentence sits at the moment of failure. The measurable check is the corpus method itself — re-read the next twenty real question sets after increment 1 lands and count clean answers against the ≈28% baseline. If the coined-label rate does not fall, the lint's pattern widens; if comprehension failure does not fall, the artefact route is not being taken and guideline 5 needs a harder edge (a server refusal on more than two questions).
- **Lint false positives.** `S3`, `H1`, `M1` (a chip size) match the code pattern. The model sees the message and rephrases; the pattern is one constant. Kept narrow deliberately — it exists to catch `D7`, not to police prose.
- **Longer question bodies.** The cold-reader contract makes a body 3–5 sentences where today's are one dense paragraph. The panel already scrolls (`min(24rem, 40dvh)`); the collapse control already exists. The trade is deliberate: words spent on the situation and one example are the words the corpus shows were missing.
- **`ChatMarkdown` in the panel.** It is a large component with cwd/environment context; the wrapper under `apps/web/src/loom/` keeps the panel hunk to a swap. If its cost in the composer drawer is visible (it renders on every open question, not on every keystroke), a lighter inline-markdown path with only code, bold and path-chips is the fallback.
- **Supersede from web changes a user habit.** Today typing in the composer and sending answers the current question. That stays; the new control is opt-in and labelled. The failure it fixes (three "I don't understand" answers to three questions) is real, and the server path already exists.
