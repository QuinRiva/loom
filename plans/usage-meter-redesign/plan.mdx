# Usage meter — designed from the hierarchy

<Callout tone="decision">
  **What you are deciding.** The shape of the sidebar usage meter, before any code. The
  argument is the table below: each decision the meter serves, the smallest signal that
  answers it, and where that signal lands. Three approaches then differ in *how* they
  communicate the same signals — not in which windows they show. Each has a `ReviewChoice`;
  the three calls the intent brief leaves open are in the `QuestionForm` at the bottom.
  **Silence defaults to the recommendation (Timeline).** A standalone real-size mockup of it
  is at `.artifacts/usage-meter-mockup-v2-20260926/index.html`.
</Callout>

## The hierarchy

Every entry traces to `intent.md`. The footer is 248 px wide; tier 1 must be read in its
first ~40 px, tier 2 must be compact all week and legible in the last 36 hours, tier 3
does not belong in the footer at all.

## Approach A — Timeline (recommended)

One wall-clock axis for everything in the 5-hour tier: the pool's window and each sub's.
The bar *is* the window — its right end is the reset, its fill the spend. A single
vertical now-line runs through all six bars: **fill past the line is over pace**, and the
gap between the line and a bar's end is its time to reset, so the staggered 5-hour clocks
read as geometry rather than as five countdowns. A ▼ marks where the current burn empties
a window when that lands before its reset — **amber** if it also lands before the end of
the working day, **hollow grey** if the day ends first. The dashed line is the end of the
working day when it falls inside the axis. The Fable line is a plain pace bar on the week.

Heights are fixed: **111 px in every state, 128 px while Codex's weekly is in
exception**. Nothing reflows as thresholds cross.

## Recommendation

Timeline. It is the only approach where the tier 1 question is answered by *position*
(where the ▼ lands relative to the bar end and the dashed line) rather than by reading
two numbers, and the only one where the five 5-hour clocks are comparable at a glance
without five countdowns — while staying fixed-height at 111 px against the rejected 150–200 px. What would change it: if the 4 px bars are unreadable at real size on your
display, or the ▼ and dashed markers do not register without the legend, Weighted rows is
the fallback (same data derivation; only the bar geometry differs).

## Data

**Time-to-empty needs a burn rate the wire does not carry.** Each account window arrives
as `usedPercent`, `resetsAt`, `windowDurationMins` every 5 minutes via
`subscribeServerConfig` (`UsageLimitSourceSnapshot`, `apps/server/src/usage/UsageLimitSources.ts`).
The rate is derived **client-side** in `apps/web/src/loom/subscriptionMeter.ts`:

- A module-level ring keyed `${account.key}:${window.id}` keeps the last **6 readings**
  (30 min) of `(checkedAt, usedPercent)`, appending only when `checkedAt` changes; it is
  cleared when `resetsAt` moves or `usedPercent` falls (a new window).
- Rate = (last − first) ÷ elapsed once **≥3 readings span ≥10 min**. Until then the
  fallback is the window-average `usedPercent ÷ elapsed hours` — exactly what the pace tick
  already shows — and the ▼ is drawn hollow to say the projection is coarse.
- Pool rate and pool fill are the means over the five subs (`poolWindows` already averages
  this way). Right after a 5-hour reset the ring empties and the window-average is ~0, so
  no marker is drawn for ten minutes; after a page reload the same ten minutes apply.
- Pace ticks use `elapsedShare()` from `packages/shared/src/usageLimits.ts`; the pool's
  tick is the mean elapsed share.

Client-side because `UsageLimitSources.ts` and `packages/contracts/src/providerUsageLimits.ts`
are upstream files (t3code #9875, #10395) that upstream is still changing; a rate field
there is a marked hunk on a contract that would conflict at every pull. If the ten-minute
blind spot after reloads proves annoying, or mobile wants the meter, the same ring moves
into a `usageBurn.loom.ts` beside the poller and publishes one `// loom:` field — about
40 lines relocated.

**End of working day** is a constant, 18:00 local, in `subscriptionMeter.ts` (question 1).

**Slice 2 — last served by, live-thread counts** (task c61fa90a, researcher report
`322ce596`): the hub names the account on every response in `X-Cpa-Trace-Id`; a
loom-injected pi extension forwards it and the server maps index → email. That gives the
thread header a "carl3@ · 4 m ago" chip and the sub popover a "3 live threads" line, both
drawn greyed in the popover above. Nothing in the footer waits on it.
