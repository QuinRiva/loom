# Usage meter — designed from the hierarchy

<Callout tone="success">
  **Accepted: Approach A — Timeline**, with four rulings folded in. (1) The meter carries

## The hierarchy

Every entry traces to `intent.md`. The footer is 248 px wide; tier 1 must be read in its
first ~40 px, tier 2 must be compact all week and legible in the last 36 hours, tier 3
does not belong in the footer at all.

## Approach A — Timeline (recommended)

One wall-clock axis for everything in the 5-hour tier: the pool's window and each sub's.
The bar *is* the window — its right end is the reset, its fill the spend. A single
vertical now-line runs through all six bars: **fill past the line is over pace**, and the
gap between the line and a bar's end is its time to reset, so the staggered 5-hour clocks
read as geometry rather than as five countdowns. An amber ▼ marks where the current burn
empties a window when that lands before its reset: its distance from the now-line is the
time to empty, its distance from the bar end the gap that would be sat out. The Fable line
is a plain pace bar on the week. Codex, a one-account pool, is the last row through the
same path, on its own axis.

Height is fixed: **128 px in every state**. Nothing reflows as thresholds cross.

@@A_STATES@@

The pair the brief asks the meter to distinguish. Both carry a ▼ before the reset; what
differs is *where*: 2.5 h from now against a 3 h reset at 10:00, 3 h from now at 16:30
(empty at 19:25). The meter says **when** the burn empties the window, never **whether**
that matters — that depends on the evening, the meeting or the gym only the human knows.

@@A_PAIR@@

Weekly exceptions in the last 36 hours: jacob@ (91%, resets in 4 h) and carl4@ (88%,
resets in 1.5 days) carry ▲; carl@ (66%, resets Sat 05:30) and caaarl@ (35%, resets in
9 h) carry ▽ — each has ≥20% to spend before a reset inside 24 h. In the real mid-week
reading only carl4@'s Fable clock (77% at 47% of the week) and Codex's exhausted weekly
earn a mark.

## Approach B — Budget as hours

The headline is a **figure**, not a bar: hours of work left in the pool at the current
burn, beside the reset. Bars shrink to 28 px pace glyphs, and the five subs become a strip
of columns with their marks above — shape only; their resets and projections live on
hover. **~100 px.** The cheapest in height and the most numeric; it loses the per-sub
reset stagger, and after a reset or a reload the figure reads "—" for ten minutes.

## Approach C — Weighted rows

Normalised 0–100 bars with the pace tick, the convention the Limits page already uses, and
rows that earn their height: a **quiet** sub is a 6 px line, a sub that diverges ≥15 pts
from the pool, resets within 30 min or will empty before its reset is **raised** to a
12 px row with its numbers, and a sub with a weekly mark is **loud** at 21 px with the
weekly reason under it. **114–165 px depending on the state.** The most attention-shaped
of the three; the cost is a footer that changes height as thresholds cross, and five bars
that cannot be compared for reset order without reading five countdowns.

## Recommendation

Timeline, accepted. It is the only approach where the tier 1 question is answered by
*position* (where the ▼ lands relative to now and to the bar end) rather than by reading
two numbers, and the only one where the five 5-hour clocks are comparable at a glance
without five countdowns — while staying fixed-height at 128 px against the rejected
150–200 px. Weighted rows remains the fallback if the 4 px bars prove unreadable at real
size (same data derivation; only the bar geometry differs).

## Data

**Pools are provider-agnostic.** A pool is every account sharing a `driver`, from whatever
source publishes it; the meter never asks which. `meterPools()` in
`apps/web/src/loom/subscriptionMeter.ts` already does this:
`collectLimitAccounts(presentations)` (`packages/shared/src/usageLimits.ts`) merges native
provider instances with every hub snapshot on `driver:email`; `splitLoomPiAccount` then
unfolds the pi instance's account-encoded windows (`accountKey:label:kind[:scope]`,
`packages/shared/src/usageWindowId.ts`) into one synthetic account per label; and
`collectLimitPools` groups the lot by driver. What the rebuild adds on top:

- **One identity per account across sources.** A hub row is keyed by email; a pi
  token-file row only by its label (`carl3`). Within a driver, rows merge on the display
  label — the email's local part, or the pi label — and the fresher `checkedAt` wins,
  the rule `collectLimitAccounts` already applies to two reports of one email. Today the
  poller's Claude arms stand down while a hub is registered (PR #268), so the merge is
  a safety rule, not a daily path; it is what lets a direct `anthropic-<tag>` provider, a
  second hub kind, or a Gemini/Grok/OpenAI multi-account source land in the same rows.
- **Window roles by `kind`, not by id.** The 5-hour tier is the pool's `session` window
  (`five_hour` from a hub, `primary` from pi, Codex's `primary` alike); the weekly is
  `weekly`; a `weekly` window carrying a model scope (`seven_day_<model>` / pi
  `secondary:<model>`) is the per-model carve-out — the Fable line when the pool reports
  one, absent otherwise. A one-account pool (Codex today) is one row on its own axis; its
  weekly shows through the mark rule and the hover, as every other row's does.
- **Nothing here depends on cli-proxy.** Remove the hub and the same rows render from
  whatever else reports.

**Time-to-empty needs a burn rate the wire does not carry.** Each account window arrives
as `usedPercent`, `resetsAt`, `windowDurationMins` every 5 minutes via
`subscribeServerConfig`. The rate is derived **client-side** in `subscriptionMeter.ts`:

- A module-level ring keyed `${account.key}:${window.id}` keeps the last **6 readings**
  (30 min) of `(checkedAt, usedPercent)`, appending only when `checkedAt` changes; it is
  cleared when `resetsAt` moves or `usedPercent` falls (a new window).
- Rate = (last − first) ÷ elapsed once **≥3 readings span ≥10 min**. Until then the
  fallback is the window-average `usedPercent ÷ elapsed hours` — exactly what the pace tick
  already shows — and the ▼ is drawn hollow to say the projection is coarse.
- Pool rate and pool fill are the means over the five subs (`poolWindows` already averages
  this way). Right after a 5-hour reset the ring empties and the window-average is ~0, so
  no marker is drawn for ten minutes; after a page reload the same ten minutes apply.
- Pace ticks use `elapsedShare()` from `packages/shared/src/usageLimits.ts`; the pool's
  tick is the mean elapsed share.

Client-side because `UsageLimitSources.ts` and `packages/contracts/src/providerUsageLimits.ts`
are upstream files (t3code #9875, #10395) that upstream is still changing; a rate field
there is a marked hunk on a contract that would conflict at every pull. If the ten-minute
blind spot after reloads proves annoying, or mobile wants the meter, the same ring moves
into a `usageBurn.loom.ts` beside the poller and publishes one `// loom:` field — about
40 lines relocated.

**Slice 2 — last served by, live-thread counts** (task c61fa90a, researcher report
`322ce596`) is the one **source-specific** piece: a cli-proxy hub names the account on
every response in `X-Cpa-Trace-Id`; a loom-injected pi extension forwards it and the
server maps index → email. That gives the thread header a "carl3@ · 4 m ago" chip and the
sub popover a "3 live threads" line, both drawn greyed in the popover above. It is optional
per source — a source that cannot name the serving account simply has no line — and
nothing in the footer waits on it.

`vp check` and `vp run typecheck` green.

## Decided

- **End of working day** — none. Rejected on its premise: the day is dynamic (extra work
  when subs are expiring unused, an early finish for a meeting or the gym), so any fixed
  time would mislead. The ▼ is pure geometry; time-to-empty appears as a clock time on
  hover.
- **Mark thresholds** — risk ▲ at ≥85% used or ≥20 pts over pace; opportunity ▽ at
  ≥20% left and the weekly resets within 24 h.
- **Codex** — always a row under the pool, through the same path as every account.
