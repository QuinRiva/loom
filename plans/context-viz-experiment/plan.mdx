# One jsonl read, one RPC, three views behind three switches, and a checklist that deletes any of them

Build a server service that reads a pi thread's session jsonl on demand and answers one report (every LLM call's context size, every item that entered the window with its exact or split token count, three findings), served by one new ws method **`loom.contextUsage`**. On the client, three per-device switches in Settings, all on from the start, gate three consumers of that one answer: per-row figures and a rail in the timeline, and a **Context** right-panel tab holding the composition list and the growth chart as sub-views, seeded into a pi thread's tab strip by the switch itself (the ring and its popover are untouched). The bets: **read the file, persist nothing** (17 to 69 ms on the 685K fixture, under 0.7 s on the largest session on this machine, and it covers every thread ever run) and **join figures to timeline rows on the client by tool-call id** (pi's `toolCallId` is Loom's `nativeItemRef.nativeId`, so the turn-item contract and the orchestrator stay untouched). The cost is about **22 marked lines across 13 upstream files**, all the rest in `apps/server/src/loom/context-viz/` and `apps/web/src/loom/context-viz/`, and the one large build is the growth chart's hand-rolled SVG.

## 2. The server: the reader, the attribution, the findings

All of it lives in a new folder, `apps/server/src/loom/context-viz/`, and nothing in it is imported from outside the folder except by `wsMethods.ts`.

### 2.1 The reader (`piSessionContext.ts`)

Reuses: the file path from `piSessionPathOf`; the streaming posture of `apps/server/src/usage/usageTranscriptReader.ts` (node:fs, line-split, `// @effect-diagnostics nodeBuiltinImport:off`). It does not reuse `readTranscriptRecords` itself, which is typed to the usage dashboard's `UsageRecord`; widening that would be an upstream hunk in two files for one consumer. The reader is `node:readline` over `createReadStream`, `JSON.parse` on each line whose first 40 bytes carry one of `"type":"message"`, `"type":"compaction"`, `"type":"usage"`, `"type":"session"`; everything else is skipped unparsed. The reference for every rule below is `.artifacts/context-viz/analyse.py` (Python, the mockups' source of numbers), and the test fixtures are derived from the two sessions it was run on.

<Steps>
  <Step at="1" title="Entries to calls and pending items">
    A `system` entry contributes one item per non-empty section in `sections` (`preamble`, `addendum`, `project_context`, `skills`, `cwd`) and one `tool_schema` item per entry of `toolsAdded` (chars = the JSON length of `{name, description, parameters}`); a second system entry mid-session (from `tool_search`) adds schemas the same way. A `user` entry is one `user_message` item, classified by prefix into `human`, `control_plane_notice` (`[T3 Workstream control plane`), `child_notification` (`Notification from thread`), `agent_brief` (`[Brief from`, `[From your parent`) or `consult`; the class is a label on the composition's sub-rows, never a finding input. A `toolResult` entry is one `tool_result` item with chars of its text blocks, the count of image blocks, `isError`, and its `toolCallId`. A `compaction` entry closes the series (step 4). A `usage` entry with `kind: "cache_warm"` is counted into totals and ignored for context. An `assistant` entry is a call: `ctx = input + cacheRead + cacheWrite`, `output`, `reasoning`, `cost` by component, `stopReason`, the toolCall blocks (id, name, arguments JSON length, a label derived as `analyse.py`'s `label_for`), and the char lengths of its thinking and text blocks.
  </Step>
  <Step at="2" title="The delta identity">
    For consecutive valid calls, `appended(N) = ctx(N) − ctx(N−1) − output(N−1)`. A call is invalid when `ctx` is 0 or `stopReason` is `error` or `aborted`; its pending items roll forward into the next valid gap. Call 0's budget is its whole `ctx`.
  </Step>
  <Step at="3" title="One item in the gap: exact; several: split">
    One pending item takes the whole budget and is `measured: true`. Several share it by character weight (chars ÷ 2.4 for tool schemas, ÷ 2.6 for tool results, ÷ 2.8 otherwise) after reserving 1,543 tokens per image block, and are `measured: false`. The identity held with a non-negative residual on all 314 gaps of the two fixtures, so a negative budget is a reader bug and is surfaced as `diagnostics.negativeGaps`, not clamped silently.
  </Step>
  <Step at="4" title="Compaction resets, forks continue">
    After a `compaction` entry the next valid call opens a new series: its `ctx` is one `compaction_base` item (`measured: true`, label "Compacted: summary and kept messages") and `prev` is reset, so the chart draws a cliff down and the composition shows the base as one row. A fork's file begins with copied history, which is read like any other history; nothing special.
  </Step>
  <Step at="5" title="What the assistant wrote stays in the window">
    Every valid call except the last contributes three items dated at the next call: `assistant_thinking` (exact, `reasoning`), and the rest of `output` split by chars between `assistant_text` and `tool_call_args` (both `measured: false`). These carry the call's message `id`, the call's index and, per text or thinking block, its content index, for the client's ordinal join (§3.4).
  </Step>
  <Step at="6" title="Carried calls and the cost to keep">
    Each item records `firstSeenCall` and `carriedCalls` (valid calls from then to the end). `costToKeepUsd` = tokens × the write unit price at its first call + tokens × the read unit price at every later call, where a call's unit prices are `cost.cacheWrite ÷ usage.cacheWrite` and `cost.cacheRead ÷ usage.cacheRead` (null when the divisor is 0). No price table on the server.
  </Step>
  <Step at="7" title="Idle gaps and cache lapses">
    Between consecutive calls more than 60 minutes apart, an `idleGap` is recorded; it is `cacheLapsed` when the later call's `cacheWrite` is at least 90% of its `ctx`, with `rewriteCostUsd = cost.cacheWrite` of that call. The fixture's one such gap cost $9.56.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### 2.4 Tests (`piSessionContext.test.ts`, `findings.test.ts`)

Two committed jsonl fixtures under the same folder, each a few KB, **synthesised** in the shape of the two real sessions (the real files are 3.3 MB and 1.3 MB of another project's commands and paths, and stay out of the tree; the fixtures use invented tool names, paths and text with the real files' usage numbers): one with the identity cases, one with a compaction. The cases, each one assertion on counts:

- a single-result gap is exact (`measured: true`, tokens equal the identity);
- two results in one gap split by chars and sum to the budget;
- an aborted call's items roll into the next gap;
- a compaction opens series 1 with one `compaction_base` item and the chart's `ctx` drops;
- a `cache_warm` entry changes `totals.cacheWarmCount` and nothing else;
- an image block reserves 1,543 tokens before the char split;
- the three rules fire on the trimmed fixture with the expected ids and not on an empty session.

No mocks: the reader takes a path, the tests write the fixture to a temp dir.

## 3. The client core: switches, store, door, tab

Everything under `apps/web/src/loom/context-viz/`. This section is what phases 3 to 5 build on and what a coder on the server phase never touches.

### 3.1 The three switches (tier 2, per device)

`LoomClientSettingsFields` and `LoomClientSettingsPatchFields` in `packages/contracts/src/settings.loom.ts` gain `contextVizTimeline`, `contextVizComposition`, `contextVizGrowth`, each `Schema.Boolean` defaulting to **`true`** (Carl: "No, turn all three on so I see everything at once"). Client settings persist per device (`localApi.ts`: browser storage, or the desktop bridge's file), the same tier as `contextWindowMeterEnabled` and `autoOpenWorkstreamPanel`; nothing syncs between devices, which is right for an experiment Carl runs on one machine. A default of `true` means a browser that has never seen the keys gets all three on, which is the chosen behaviour; a later flip of the default to `false` is one line.

The Settings rows (`ContextVizSettingsRows.tsx`: a "Context diagnostics" group of three `SettingsRow` with a `Switch`, a reset button each, and the "changed labels" and "restore defaults" spreads) are rendered **from the existing loom-owned** `apps/web/src/loom/LoomAutoOpenSettingsRows.tsx`, which already sits inside `SettingsPanels.tsx` with its own three marked lines; it widens its `Pick<UnifiedSettings, …>` and spreads the new helpers. That is three lines in a loom file and **no new upstream line** for Settings. The group therefore lands in **Settings → Behavior beside Auto-open workstream**, not beside "Context window indicator": that switch lives in the Legacy features section (`LegacyFeaturesSection` in `SettingsPanels.tsx`), where an experiment's switches do not belong, and placing them there would cost an upstream line for a worse home.

### 3.2 The door: the tab follows the switch (no upstream line, no popover change)

Carl: "Settings only, keep the popover as it is." So the ring, its popover and `ContextSpend.tsx` are untouched, and the Context tab is **seeded** into a pi thread's right panel by the switch, through the seed path the tiers doc reserves for exactly this ("if a second auto-opened surface is ever added, the same action seeds both and activates exactly one by an explicit priority"):

- `seedWorkstreamSurface` in the loom-owned `apps/web/src/loom/seedRightPanelSurfaces.ts` becomes `seedLoomSurfaces(current, { workstream, context })`: it adds each eligible surface the thread does not yet have, and on a thread with **no panel state at all** opens the panel on Workstream if eligible, else Context; on a thread with any state it adds the tabs and touches neither `activeSurfaceId` nor `isOpen` (the non-activating seed). The store action `seedWorkstream` in `rightPanelStore.ts` (two lines that are already loom hunks) passes the eligibility through; it runs the reducer under `automaticUpdate`, which is the point: an automatic transition bumps none of the user-action revisions.
- Eligibility for `context` is: the active provider thread's driver is `pi` and `contextVizComposition || contextVizGrowth`. It is computed in `useLoomRightPanelSurfaces.ts` beside `workstreamRoot`, in the effect that already runs the seed, and recorded in `workstreamUiStore.ts`'s per-thread `AutoOpenedSurfaces` flag as `context: true`, so the seed fires **at most once per thread** and a tab Carl closes stays closed for that thread, with the `+` menu entry ("Context", available under the same eligibility) as the explicit way back.
- **The way out, through the same automatic action, never `closeSurface`.** `closeSurface` is a user action: it runs through `closeAction` → `userAction`, which bumps `userActionRevisionByThreadKey` and `closeRevisionByThreadKey` even when the tab is absent, and upstream's proactive Diff and PR opener (`openProactive`) refuses to open once that revision has moved; a real close also lands in `closedViewStore`'s reopen history. Using it would mean that with the switches off, merely visiting a thread suppressed the automatic Diff tab, breaking "all three off is today's Loom exactly". So the reducer is the way out too: with `context: false`, `seedLoomSurfaces` removes a present `context` surface, mirroring what `closeSurface`'s updater does to `surfaces`, `activeSurfaceId` and `isOpen` but under `automaticUpdate`, with no revision bump and no reopen-history entry. The effect calls `seedWorkstream` with `context: false` **only when the thread holds the surface or its flag is set** (a thread with neither is untouched, so an off-switch visit writes nothing), and clears the flag so a later switch-on seeds again. Other threads' persisted tabs go as each is visited.

`docs/architecture/loom-ui-state-tiers.md` says the Workstream tab is the only seeded surface in two places; P2 amends those sentences to name Context (and the removal reverts them).

### 3.3 The store and the loader (`contextUsageStore.ts`)

Pattern: `apps/web/src/loom/threadSpend.ts` (a store filled by `executeAtomQuery` with `{ refresh: true }`, no atom family in the components). A zustand store keyed by `scopedThreadKey` holding `{ status: "idle" | "loading" | "ready" | "none", report, refreshKey }`. `useContextUsageLoader(threadRef)` is mounted by whichever consumer is on (the timeline host, the Context tab, or both; a single-flight guard by key + refreshKey means two mounts cost one request): it reads `useQuietThreadProjection(threadRef)` (the structure held still through streamed text; `useThreadProjection` updates on every streamed token and would re-render the host and the rail per token during a running turn), derives `refreshKey` as the `updatedAt` of the newest provider turn whose status is terminal (`completed`, `failed`, `cancelled`, `interrupted`) and the active provider thread's driver, and when the driver is `pi` and the key changes, runs `loomCommands.contextUsage({ environmentId, input: { threadId } })` and writes the result. The query family entry goes beside `threadSpend` in `packages/client-runtime/src/state/loom/goalCommands.ts` (loom-owned; `staleTimeMs: 0`, since the store owns freshness). This is the folder's one `useEffect`, budgeted in `scripts/effect-budget.json` under its own path. Consumers read with selectors, so 184 row figures re-render once per answer, not per projection change. Because the switches default on, every pi thread visited pays one request on its first open; the store keeps the eight most recently visited threads' reports (about 150 KB raw each at the fixture's size) and drops the rest, so switching between threads does not re-read a file the server just parsed, and memory stays bounded.

### 3.4 The join (`join.ts`, pure, with one fixture test)

The timeline's rows know Loom items; the report knows jsonl entries. Three lookups, built once per report and memoised by reference:

- **Tool results, exact by id.** `item.nativeItemRef.nativeId` is pi's `toolCallId` (`baseItemFields(turn, toolCallId, …)` in the adapter), and a `tool_result` item's `id` is the same string. A `Map<string, LoomContextItem>`.
- **Runs to calls, by anchors.** A Loom run's tool items name the calls that issued them; a run owns every call from its first anchored call to the call before the next run's first anchor. Runs with no tool call (prose-only replies) take the unanchored calls between their neighbours by order. This gives each run its `startCtx`, `endCtx`, `addedTokens` and `costUsd` for the turn header, and the Heaviest menu's candidates.
- **Assistant text and thinking, by ordinal.** A Loom `assistant_message` or `reasoning` item's `nativeItemRef.nativeId` is `<providerTurnId>:m<ordinal>:c<contentIndex>`; within the run's calls, the `ordinal`-th call's `assistant_text` or `assistant_thinking` item with that `contentIndex` is the figure. When the run's call count and its item ordinals disagree (a steer mid-turn, an interrupted stream), the figure is withheld rather than guessed; the test asserts that on the fixture no tool row is withheld and counts the prose rows that are.

The user message that opens a run takes the `user_message` item dated just before the run's first call. Steers mid-turn show no figure.

### 3.5 The Context tab (one new right-panel kind)

A `context` singleton surface, added the way `workstream` was: `RIGHT_PANEL_KINDS`, the `RightPanelSurface` union and the `singletonSurface` switch in `rightPanelStore.ts` (3 marked lines), the label and icon cases in `RightPanelTabs.tsx` (2), the lazy import and the render case in `RightPanelSurfaceContent.tsx` (2), the persisted-entry guard in `closedViewStore.ts` (1), plus `LOOM_SURFACES.context` in the loom-owned `seedRightPanelSurfaces.ts` and a "Context" entry in `useLoomRightPanelSurfaces.ts`'s `+` menu list, available while either panel switch is on. It is seeded only as §3.2 describes (by the switch, once per thread, never as a focus steal) and never because a number got large. Its open state is tier 1 (persisted per thread by `rightPanelStore`).

`ContextPanel.tsx` reads the two panel switches and renders a header (title, "as of the latest call, N calls, M turns, model", a Refresh button, and the **Composition | Growth** sub-view switch when both are on), then the sub-view that is on. The sub-view choice is **tier 4**, component state defaulting to Composition (Carl: "Forgotten on close (component state); the tab opens on Composition"); with both switches off the tab is being closed by §3.2's effect, and in the one render before that it shows a single line naming the switches.

Two lines of hygiene so removal leaves no tombstone. First, in the build: the persisted-state migrate in `rightPanelStore.ts` gains `if (!RIGHT_PANEL_KINDS.includes(kind)) return []; // loom: drop retired kinds` ahead of the retired-kind lines that already exist (`artifact`, `graph`); it is general and stays. Second, at removal: zustand's persist runs `migrate` **only when the stored version differs** from `RIGHT_PANEL_STORAGE_VERSION` (16 today, and the store has no `merge` override), so the removal also bumps that constant to 17 on its existing line, which makes every browser's persisted `context` surfaces pass through the guard once. Without the bump, every pi thread ever seeded (most of them, with the switches on by default) would keep a `{ kind: "context" }` surface that `RightPanelTabs` can neither label nor draw: a blank tab, or a blank panel where it was active. The version line's own comment already records the convention for the collision this creates ("the next upstream bump takes 17" becomes 18); at the next pull the resolution is upstream's number plus one, with our guard folded in.

- **The payload is 49.4 KB deflated, not 12 KB.** The real report for the 58-turn thread is 311 KB raw over 656 items; structure, not labels, is the weight.
