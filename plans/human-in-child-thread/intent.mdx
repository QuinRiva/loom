# Talking inside a child thread: all three faults still happen, and each has a small fix of its own

All three failures the retro reported **still happen on today's code**. None was fixed in the meantime and none is a regression. Each is the control plane doing what it was built to do, in a situation it was not built for. They do not share a cause, so the recommendation is **three small fixes and no new "in conversation" state**. The table shows what changes in each situation.

<Callout tone="info" title="What the parent is told" focus>
  Nothing about your conversation, as now. It hears from the child, in one message quoting you, when a decision of yours changes what it would do or a finished child's report changes materially. It gets one notice, not two, when a child's work is finished and waiting on your approval. Its part is then to point you at that child, not to carry your corrections to it. It stops getting "went quiet" notices about a child it launched for you to talk to.
</Callout>

**Goal.** "I do this occasionally, and there clearly isn't a compatible control plane state that supports it." Make it unremarkable to work directly inside a child. Nothing you, the child or its parent see should describe it as a fault. What you decide there should reach the parent when it matters, and the record when it does not.

**You'll know it works when.**

- A planner child finishes a brief that needs your approval. The child shows **Awaiting acceptance**, its parent gets one notice saying the work is finished and held for you, and nothing queued behind it has started. The parent tells you which child is waiting, with a link.
- You write your corrections in that child. It revises and is still held; the parent hears nothing about the round. You write "approved" there. It completes, its report quotes what you changed, and the parent gets the ordinary finished notice.
- At no point does the parent ask you to approve in its own thread, or pass your words on to the child.
- A parent launches a child for you to walk through results with. Over two days you reply at your own pace, the parent sends it a note, and one of its own children finishes. The child never shows **Yielded** and the parent never hears "went quiet".
- You write to a finished child and it does more work for you. It stays finished, its hand-back succeeds and adds a dated section to its report, and work that depended on it is untouched. The parent gets one message from the child saying what changed.
- You ask a finished child to clarify something in its report, and it tidies the report to match. The parent hears nothing.
- In that child you say "get the parent to delegate this". The parent receives one message quoting you, on the child's first attempt.
- **Must not:**
  - wake the parent because you asked a child a question;
  - reopen a finished child, or re-block work behind it, because you wrote to it;
  - stop reporting a child nobody is talking to that ends its turn without handing back;
  - let work queued behind a deliverable start before you approve it;
  - break the child's message to its parent that quotes you.

**Approaches in play.**

The overall shape:

- **Fix each where it happens, with no state that switches on and off.** _The pick._ The one fact the fixes share, "a person has written in this child", is already worked out by the server every turn; it is what unlocks the child's ask-you-a-question tool. Wins if the three stay as unrelated as they are in the code.
- **One stored "in conversation with you" state on the child**, switched on by your first message, shown as a badge and to the parent, and driving all three behaviours. Wins if you want to see it on the board. Its cost: a state needs an end, and nothing marks when a conversation is over. The walk-through child sat three days between two of your messages.

Finished, waiting on your approval:

- **One hand-back that means "finished, held for approval", and you correct and approve in the child.** _The pick._ It mirrors the hand-back that already exists for "I need a person".
- Dropped: the child simply completes and the parent holds the approval. It makes the parent the relay for your corrections, loses the **Awaiting acceptance** marker and lets queued work start before you approve.
- Keep the two calls and merge the two notices. Leaves the child inventing a word for its state, as 44 did.

Waiting for your reply:

- **Only a child the parent declares, at launch, as one for you to talk to is never reported as quiet.** _Your pick._
- Dropped: any child you have written to, until it next hands back. A working child you steered that later stalls would be reported to nobody; the cluster-review coder you steered nine times on 26 Oct would have been one.
- Dropped: leave it as it is.

After it is finished:

- **The refused hand-back is accepted as an addition to the report. The parent is not woken for it, and the child tells the parent when the change is material.** _The pick._ Nine finished children tried exactly this. It is where your decisions go when the parent does not need them now.
- Dropped: every addition appears in the parent's next routine digest. It wakes the parent for a tidied sentence.
- Message the parent only, with no addition to the report. Fewest parts. Loses the record: 56 of the 132 messages came after the child finished.
- Dropped: your message reopens the finished child. A question would un-finish work and re-block what was behind it.

**Decided.**

- "I think this is something that we should immediately act on."
- The parent is not told about a conversation: "the entire reason I engaged with the subthread is that that was the thread that was doing the work and I didn't need to pollute the context of the parent with unnecessary details." And: "unless the parent thread would get value from immediately being made aware of my comment, it's usually sufficient for the child to simply include that information in its summary report." Both 23 Oct, when the automatic digest of your messages to the parent was removed.
- The child does not message its parent every time you write: "I don't want the result being instead of a control plane message, we get a notify_thread from the child on *every* interaction instead (that would be worse)." Same day. Loading that tool for more children must not produce this.
- The child's message to its parent, quoting you, is the supported way to hand a decision up: "Get the parent to delegate out this work, and we can continue going through the results."
- A finished child you write to keeps its tools and stays finished. The 31 Jul plan that removed the read-only mode records this as your decision; it does not quote you.
- You correct and approve a held deliverable in the child that made it: "shouldn't I just be communicating directly with the planner in these situations? Why should I be engaging with the orchestrator to have it just relay messages to the planner?" 10 Nov.
- Only a child the parent launched for you to talk to, and declared so at launch, is never reported as gone quiet. Your answer of 10 Nov.
- A finished child's addition to its report does not wake the parent unless it is material: "we shouldn't be waking a parent for minor changes to a report", and "the parent probably *does* need to be informed, if the changes are material." 10 Nov. You also noted that some additions are not things you asked for: "I've asked the agent to clarify something in the report, and then it has taken it upon itself to update the report to carry those clarifications."
- _Assumed:_ the child judges what is material, by the test it already applies before messaging its parent: the parent would act differently, or something the parent relied on in the report has changed.
- _Assumed:_ the parent still reads a held deliverable against its brief, and sends the child back if something is plainly wrong, before it points you at the child. For this brief that check confirmed two quotes attributed to you and corrected one count. Redirect if you would rather be pointed at the child at once.
- _Assumed:_ the approval hold stays as the thing that blocks queued work, rather than moving the wait to the parent; redirect if wrong.
- _Assumed:_ the tool for messaging the parent loads for any child once a person has written to it, the same moment its question tool unlocks.
- _Assumed:_ a finished child nobody has written to still has its hand-back refused, with a refusal that says what to do instead.

**Not doing.** A badge or board state for "in conversation". Any automatic summary of your messages to the parent. Reopening finished children. The 43 other "matched no route" hand-backs that are not about approval (`merge_conflict`, `paused`). New screens: the visible changes are the wording of two notices in the parent's timeline and the parent's message pointing you at a held child.

**Questions for you.** None open. Your answers of 10 Nov are under Decided.

- Will a child tell a material change to its report from a tidied one the way you would? An eval case with one of each settles it.

<Details summary="Why each still happens: the cause in the reviewed run, and today's code">

**Finished, waiting on approval.** The planner did what its instructions say. The planner role tells it to raise the approval hold "instead of completing" for an intent brief. The hold's tool description says "To hand back a report while the flag stands, submit with a short non-'done' outcome." It raised the hold, then submitted with a word it made up.

Any word the control plane does not know is treated as an unplanned hand-back, and the hold and the hand-back each send their own notice. The parent then sent corrections twice. A parent's message clears the hold, so the child raised and submitted again each time. That is the three pairs of notices on 3 Nov. Nothing here has changed since.

**Waiting for your reply.** The check for "gone quiet" exempts a turn only when your message started it. On 5 and 6 Nov the walk-through child ended three turns that something else had started. The first was its launch; your first message arrived 48 seconds in and joined that turn. The second was the parent's note telling it to ignore the first notice. The third was a notice that one of its own children had finished. Ten minutes after each, it was reported.

The fix that landed on 5 Nov was for two other false reports (after a stop, and while a child's own child was finishing), not this one.

**After it is finished.** A finished thread's hand-back is refused on purpose, so that a late hand-back cannot re-run finished work; the 31 Jul plan kept that guard. The refusal names no alternative. The child's standing instructions tell it to message its parent "only when it would act differently now". That tool is loaded only for orchestrators, so a coder's or planner's first call fails. Of the 9 refused children, some then messaged the parent. One reasoned "I'm not notifying the parent, since the thread is finished".

</Details>
