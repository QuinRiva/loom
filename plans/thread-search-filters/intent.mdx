# Thread search you can widen or narrow, with sub-threads as results you can open

**Goal.** "I think we need to consider how search is designed - because I can imagine I'd want to be able to have filters (include vs exclude children for example)." Make search something you steer. Three visible switches say where it looks: sub-threads, archived, one project. With none touched it reads top-level threads and every report, leaves archived threads and sub-threads out, and lists nothing when nothing matches. **Switching sub-threads on lists the sub-thread itself and opens it.** A switch that only read their text, as the October toggle would have, adds little on the two rough measurements I have.

The 92% is generous in two ways. It does not check where the parent ranks, or whether it is among the rows a screen shows. And titles are the easy case: a sub-thread's report tends to repeat them, where words from deep inside its messages would score lower.

<Callout tone="decision" title="What that means for your idea" focus>
  Filters are worth having, but "include children" as a reading switch looks like the weak version. On these figures a sub-thread's words mostly sit in text search already reads, chiefly its report. What is certainly missing is seeing which sub-thread it was and landing in it. So this brief asks for three switches, sub-thread rows you can open, and a default that leaves archived threads out and stops padding. Your answers of 23 Nov are folded in below, and you approved it on 24 Nov with "Go".
</Callout>

**You'll know it works when.**

- You touch no switch, and Cmd+K, the sidebar search and the phone's thread search read a top-level thread's own messages, purpose and goal, and every sub-thread's report. No archived thread and no sub-thread is listed.
- You switch **Sub-threads** on and type `Word to PDF without malforming`: the sub-thread is listed under its top-level thread, and choosing it opens the sub-thread. Switch it off and the row is gone.
- You switch **Archived** on and archived threads join the list, marked. You switch on the project chip in Cmd+K, which is the project of the thread you have open, or pick a project in the sidebar box. Either way you get a full list from that project, not what is left of thirty rows chosen across every project.
- You choose a result whose excerpt says "Report:" and land in the sub-thread that wrote it.
- You type `xylophone marmalade wombat` and see "No threads found". <Sidenote>Those three words are now written in this brief's own threads and reports, so on the cockpit they rightly find this workstream. The check uses three fresh words nobody has written.</Sidenote> A paraphrase still works: `how do I stop the model picker showing duplicate names` lists "This model picker is awful…" at or near the top.
- You type `CI` and get message matches.
- What is switched on shows beside the search box on every screen, and one action puts it back to the default.
- An agent's `thread_search` takes the same narrowing as parameters.
- **Must not:**
  - change which live top-level threads you find with no switch touched, beyond the padding going;
  - take longer to show results than today, or send more over the socket than the thirty rows a search returns now; <Sidenote>Six agent searches traced on the cockpit, 23 Nov. Three run alone took 0.44 to 0.52 s; three sent together each waited the 2 s limit and came back with word matches only.</Sidenote>
  - leave a switch on out of sight, so a later search silently misses things;
  - go back over old threads to re-read them.

Where the switches sit. You judge this one by trying it. The pictures are Cmd+K; the sidebar box comes with it, since those are the two you search from, and the phone's search field follows the same choice.

- **Chips under the search box.** Wins if you want to see and flip a switch at a glance, on a phone as well. Costs one row of height. The words you type stay plain, which matters because they also drive title matching and highlighting.
- **Typed in the query.** Wins if you never want to leave the keyboard, and it is the same on every screen. Loses on a phone keyboard and has to be remembered. It could come later as shorthand that flips the chips.
- **A filter button with a menu.** Wins if a chip row crowds the phone. Loses because what is on hides behind a count.
- **Sub-thread rows.** Chosen: one row per matching sub-thread under its top-level thread. How many one workstream may show before the rest fold away is yours to judge when you try it. The build starts at three, with one row that unfolds the rest.
- **Stopping the padding.** Chosen: drop results matched only by meaning that are not close enough. The cut-off was set on the 57 test searches and 16 nonsense ones. No test search loses its thread from the first ten, and 14 of the 16 nonsense searches list nothing by meaning. Dropped: a separate "Related" group under the word matches. "No threads found" needs a cut-off either way, and with one the extra heading changes nothing about what is listed.
- **Speed.** Chosen: both arrive together, as today, and flipping a switch on the same words answers in about a tenth of a second. Dropped: showing word matches first. For rows to hold still, matches by meaning would have to sit below the word matches. That is a different order from the ranking you ruled stays as it is.
- Dropped: wiring the October toggle as ruled, where sub-threads' text is read and only top-level threads are listed. It changed 1 first place in 57, and it still leaves you at the top of the parent. You chose sub-threads as results instead. <Sidenote>Pull 13 decision log, ranker test of 26 Oct. Every answer in that set is a top-level thread, so it understates what the toggle adds.</Sidenote>
- Dropped: upstream's own search. Pull 16 re-checked it: it reads the messages of about 650 active top-level threads and nothing archived, no sub-thread and no report.

**Decided.** Your words, oldest first; the newest governs.

- 1 Oct, on sub-threads as results: "I can't imagine that I would *ever* want to find a sub-thread this way … enabling sub-threads to appear in the result is just going to clutter the result with things I'm not looking for."
- 26 Oct, on what you look for: "if I'm looking for something it's almost certainly something I've seen before: a root message or a report/plan I've read". The record of that ruling puts sub-thread content behind an "include sub-threads" toggle; that phrase is the runbook's, not yours.
- 26 Oct, on agents: "it's possible that when an agent uses the tool, it may get value from finding/searching sub-threads".
- 26 Oct, on ranking: "Loom hybrid, as it stands".
- 23 Nov: "all the stuff here that's trying to support "old" threads, or backport stuff to threads I'm never going to touch, is safe to drop".
- 23 Nov, on a sub-thread result: "The sub-thread itself, listed under its top-level thread, and choosing it opens the sub-thread". With the switch on, this replaces the 1 Oct line.
- 23 Nov, on the switches: sub-threads, archived, one project. "I would almost always want to exclude archived threads."
- 23 Nov, on what search reads with no switch touched: "top-level threads and every report, not sub-threads or archived. If I've archived a thread - that means I have manually identified that thread as low value and something i'm unlikely to ever need to look at, so including it in search results by default seems counterproductive." This replaces "archived included" from 26 Oct. Loom also archives two kinds of helper thread by itself, handoff drafters (91 of the 365 archived top-level threads) and document-query threads; they leave the default list too.
- 23 Nov, on plans. While you took them to be costly: "Adding plans and reports to the search is probably not that valuable, so it sounds like it's probably not worth doing." Then, told that reports are searched today: "If it's already built than that's great - plans/reports/recaps/intent briefs/explainers should all be treated the same." Reports are built and stay. The other four are not, and told what they would cost: "OK - no let's leave out all the mdx docs then."
- 23 Nov, on where you search: the sidebar search box and Cmd+K.
- 24 Nov, on the project switch in Cmd+K, asked whether it should offer any project or only the project of the thread you have open: "Current project chip only". With no thread open there is no project chip, and any other project is searched from the sidebar box and its picker.
- _Assumed:_ the agents' search keeps reading sub-threads and archived threads by default and gains the same switches as parameters; it can already switch archived off. Redirect if wrong.
- _Assumed:_ a switch lasts while the search box is in use and returns to the default when you clear or close it, so a forgotten one never hides results. The sidebar's project picker keeps its own memory, as today.
- _Assumed:_ the short-word fix is folded in, as you allowed; it is one condition.
- _Assumed:_ nothing is built before upstream pull 16 merges. It rewrites the same Cmd+K, sidebar and phone files, and removes nothing this brief relies on.

**Not doing.** Searching plans, intent briefs, recaps and explainers. Changing how results are ranked, apart from that cut-off. Re-reading or back-filling old threads. Searching tool calls, command output or an agent's messages other than its last of each turn. The find box inside a thread. The title-only pickers (Link to thread on a pull request, a scheduled task's destination, `!` in the composer). A settings page for any of it.

**Still unknown.**


- How often search fails you, and on which kind of miss. Nothing counts searches. Counting them for two weeks after the first cut settles it: what was typed, on which screen, which switches were on, whether a result was opened.
- How much reading sub-threads' text adds for words from deep inside them, and whether the right sub-thread then comes first. Neither figure above measures it. A dozen searches written from words that live only inside sub-threads' messages, run through the real search, settle both.
- Why three searches sent together each waited 2 s and came back without their matches by meaning. Timing runs on 24 Nov ruled out both first guesses. Three sent together took about half a second each. Sixty calls at once to Vertex all answered in under half a second. A cold start took about one second. A timeout logs nothing today; the build adds a log line, and the cause waits for it to happen again.
- Whether a chip row fits the phone's search field without pushing the first result off screen. Agents cannot run the phone app on this machine, so that check is yours.
