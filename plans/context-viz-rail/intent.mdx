# The rail: make it the scrollbar, put it on the minimap, move it to the left gutter, or drop it

**Goal.** "I like the idea of having it in the scrollbar track, but I think we'd need to rethink how the scrollbar *works* if we were to do that." What broke last round's track option: **the scrollbar, the minimap and the rail map three different things**. The scrollbar maps the pixels of the turns loaded so far; the minimap one strip per human prompt loaded, evenly spaced, left gutter, pointer only, gone under two prompts (so gone on every one-turn child); the rail the tokens of the whole session. Tokens under a pixel thumb was a thumb pointing at nothing. Each direction below picks one space and stays in it.

**You'll know it works when.**

- Timeline on, on your 58-turn thread: from the chat, without opening a panel, you can see where the window went, hover a segment for its name and tokens, and click to land on that row, paging back if its turn is not loaded.
- On a one-turn child, where most of the work happens, the same view is there, call by call.
- Nothing sits over the scrollbar thumb, the details card or the prose; whatever thumb there is can be grabbed at its full width.
- Timeline off: the native scrollbar, the minimap and both gutters are exactly today's Loom, with nothing reserved.
- **Must not:** animate; cost a frame on scroll; change the minimap when the switch is off; send anything new over the socket. (These carry over from the context-viz brief.)

### The scrollbar rethink, close up

  width={700}

The token-space thumb is the design's one bet. Today's rail already outlines the turn being read in token space (the outline at the foot of panel 3 above); the thumb is that outline widened to the rows in view, which `MessagesTimeline.tsx` already computes for the minimap on every scroll and does not yet hand over. A drag moves through tokens, so it lands on the row holding the token under the pointer; a wheel scroll moves through pixels, so the thumb jumps when a folded turn passes. Both are the same fact shown two ways. Hiding the native scrollbar drops its 6 px gutter on both edges, and the timeline's padding already subtracts the measured gutter, so the prose does not move when the switch flips.

The minimap today is not a map of the scroll and not a map of the thread: it is the human prompts loaded so far (`maxUserTurns: 10` in the history page policy), spaced 8 px apart whatever their size, centred in the left gutter, on fine pointers only, and hidden with fewer than two prompts. Weighting its strips answers "which of these ten turns was heavy", which the turn headers already answer inline.
