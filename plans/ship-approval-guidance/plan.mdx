# Agents asked "ship it?" because their prompt said shipping needs your approval

Of the **31** "ship it?" question cards from October whose sessions I read, **29** trace in the agent's own reasoning to one requirement: that shipping needs your approval. <Sidenote>Section 1 quotes each; the full table is in its drill-down.</Sidenote> That requirement sat in three places every turn: `AGENTS.md`, the orchestrator role and the SHIPPING POLICY block. The question tool said only "do not ask for approval you do not need". Approval the prompt said you *did* need was fair game, and a tool whose purpose is getting decisions from you was the obvious way to get it.

The proposal: say once, in the SHIPPING POLICY block, whose act shipping is and what a hand-back looks like instead. Delete the five "needs approval" phrasings and the prohibition added on 26 October. Net **−72 words** across seven files; every surface the context-limit test caps gets smaller.

**The mechanism.** On every turn in October an orchestrator read these four sentences together. Three say approval is required; the fourth tells it which approvals it may ask for.

- `AGENTS.md`: "Do not ship until the user has approved shipping the change." (since July)
- `roles/orchestrator.md`: "Ship only after approval, and never from memory" (since 30 September; before that "Once the human approves shipping, either hand the release to a `shipper` child or ship inline")
- SHIPPING POLICY block, every thread: "once the work is approved and required checks pass, an agent may land it on the main branch"
- `ask_user_question`: "do not ask to reconfirm scope or for approval you do not need"

From 19 to 26 October the orchestrator role also said, of a hand-back, "offer that as an option in your approval question". The approval question was named as the hand-back's form. Thirteen of the 31 cards were raised with that line in the prompt. PR #451 replaced it with "readiness line" on 26 October and added the prohibition.

**What the agents did with it.** Fifteen of the 31 had written the requirement into their own task tree before the card ("Ship after Carl approves", "Carl approves; ship the branch via pnpm ship"). The card was the way to tick the task. Three (S5, S117, S120) had already been told "proceed" or "let's go with 1" and raised the card anyway, reasoning that design approval and shipping approval were separate acts. The prohibition could not have changed that reasoning; only the requirement could.

## 3. What stays and why

- **The policy itself.** You say ship; an agent never merges on its own under `human` authority and never without your word under `agent` authority. It now lives in the SHIPPING POLICY block alone.
- **The hand-back line in the orchestrator role** (line 44): "offer that in your readiness line ... the diff or unmerged PR, plus the command that lands it". It describes the output, which is the positive form the skill asks for.
- **The human-only SHIPPING POLICY branch.** Unchanged; it already ends in a hand-back and carries no "approved".
- **The deploy paragraph in AGENTS.md** ("a separate, human-initiated act; never deploy ... unless the user explicitly asks"). It is a fact about whose act deploying is, with a prohibition attached. S56 and S126 show it producing "ship, or ship and deploy?" cards in the same way. The paragraph is out of this change's scope; worth a look if the deploy cards continue after this lands.
- **`pnpm ship --help`** ("Land approved work on main"): tool output read only while already shipping. Left alone.
- **The `awaiting_acceptance` and intent-brief approval lines** in the roles: they are about a child's hold and a brief's gate, not shipping.

## 4. The check, and what the result means

This is a shared-surface change (the policy block reaches every thread), so the skill's protocol wants a pre-registered hypothesis and a read of what flips.

**Hypothesis.** Ship-type question cards (header or options containing ship, merge or land) stay at zero over the 30 days after the deploy. Baseline: 34 of 128 sets in October under the requirement alone; 0 of 8 sets, across 20 merges, since the prohibition was deployed on 28 October. The query is the audit's, against the `user_input_request` turn items, and takes a minute to run.

**What it could displace.** An agent that no longer reads "needs approval" might merge without your word. The check reads each merged PR's root thread for a human message in the hour before the merge. <Sidenote>The root thread comes from the commit's `Loom-Thread` trailer.</Sidenote> Today's baseline is not clean. Of the 20 merges since 28 October, 8 follow a literal "ship it"; the rest follow "proceed", "agree with all", or answers given in a document. The signal to watch for is a merge with no human message at all in that window.

**Reading the result.** Cards return: the cause was not fully found, and the next read is of those cards' reasoning, not a restored prohibition. Cards stay at zero and merges still follow your word: done. A merge with no word from you: the policy sentence is not landing, and the first thing to read is that thread's hand-back.

**Verification before the PR.** `vp test run apps/server/src/loom/prompt/agentContextLimit.test.ts` for the byte caps on the orchestrator prompt, the coder prompt and `AGENTS.md`; every one shrinks. Then `vp check`, `vp run typecheck`, and a rendered prompt for one root thread before and after, with the two policy blocks diffed by eye.
